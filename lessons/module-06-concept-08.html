Every fact has
a paper trail.

Concept 07 gave POLICY-REFUND-001 a stable identity that survives its own edits. This concept asks three questions Concept 07 never had to: WHERE did that policy text actually come from, WHICH steps did it pass through on the way into the index, and WHEN did each of those events really happen? PROGRESSIVE BEGINNER CORE

Where it came from, what it passed through, when it happened

Concept 06 asked whether two records existing right now were really the same thing. Concept 07 asked how one document's own content changes across its version history. This concept asks a third, different question: for any single fact sitting in the index, can you point to the exact source it came from, the exact chain of stages it passed through to get here, and the exact moments each of those things happened?

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Three separate questions, bundled into one concept

THIS CONCEPT

Provenance — WHERE a fact came from: which source, which author or team. Lineage — the chain of transformation STEPS a record passed through: source → parse → clean → dedup → version, spanning Concepts 02 through 07. Timestamps — WHEN each of those events happened: source-created, source-last-modified, ingested.

NOT THIS CONCEPT

Concept 07's within-one-doc_id version history. That story is reused here as one link in the larger chain — it is not re-taught from scratch.

First memory: provenance answers WHERE, lineage answers WHICH STEPS, timestamps answer WHEN. Three different questions with three different fields, because a real audit needs all three answered at once, not just one of them.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Authority needs an input, debugging needs a trail, audits need a clock

Concept 01's auth(s) = (type_rank(source_type), −recency) formula has been running since Module 06 opened — but it has never had anywhere to get source_type and recency FROM. This concept is exactly where those two fields get captured and named: they are provenance fields, and authority is simply a formula that consumes them, not a separate thing that reinvents them. Without a lineage chain, a bad chunk surfacing at query time cannot be traced back to which single stage — parsing, cleaning, or dedup — actually introduced the problem. And without timestamps, a question as basic as "is this evidence still fresh" or "who is accountable for this fact, and since when" has no real answer at all.

05 · ARCHITECTURE / FLOW

Captured alongside every earlier stage, not reconstructed after the fact

Concept 06 — dedup + canonicalization↓Concept 07 — identity and versioning↓Concept 08 — provenance, lineage and timestamps↓Module 07 — chunking

This concept's fields are not bolted onto a finished record after the fact. Every stage from Concept 02 onward appends its own lineage event live, as it runs — the parser logs its own event when it parses, the cleaner logs its own event when it cleans, and so on. This concept's job is naming that accumulating trail and the source-side facts sitting above it, not reconstructing it after the fact from logs — that reconstruction path exists too, but only as a fallback (Section 06's backfill lane, in the advanced material).

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

What this concept is not

TermIs NOTWhy it matters
Provenance (this concept)Authority (Concept 01)Provenance supplies source_type and recency; authority is the ranking formula that consumes those two fields. Confusing the two makes it sound like there are two competing rules instead of one rule fed by one set of facts.
Lineage (this concept, the step CHAIN)Versioning (Concept 07, WITHIN one doc_id only)Concept 07's version history is one link inside this concept's larger multi-stage chain, not a competing concept. Lineage spans Concepts 02 through 07; versioning is only Concept 07's own piece of it.
This concept's 3 timestamps (source-side + pipeline-side)Concept 07's 2 timestamps (pipeline-side only)effective_at/superseded_at track a version's own lifespan inside the pipeline. source_created_at and source_last_modified_at track the SOURCE's own history, upstream of the pipeline entirely — a distinction the ingestion-lag metric depends on.
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"Provenance and authority are the same thing."

False — authority is a formula; provenance is one of its inputs. A source can have excellent provenance metadata and still rank low on authority if its source_type is weak.

"Lineage just means where it came from."

False — that's provenance alone. Lineage is the whole multi-stage trail a record passed through, not just its starting point.

"ingested_at and source_last_modified_at are always close together."

False — the gap between them (ingestion_lag) is itself a useful staleness signal. A large gap means the pipeline is slow to notice real-world edits, exactly what this concept's lab measures.

09 · WHAT YOU SHOULD UNDERSTAND

Readiness check before moving on

Provenance feeds authority, it doesn't replace it

source_type and recency are provenance fields; auth(s) is the formula that ranks sources using them.

Lineage is the whole chain, not just the start

Provenance names where a fact started; lineage names every stage it passed through afterward, Concepts 02 through 07 included.

Three timestamps, not two

source_created_at and source_last_modified_at live upstream of the pipeline; ingested_at is where Concept 07's effective_at re-enters this concept's own vocabulary.

The gap itself is a signal

ingestion_lag = ingested_at − source_last_modified_at measures how quickly the pipeline notices real-world change — not a fixed, always-small number.

Operational objectives

STATE

State the provenance-to-authority relationship

Authority's inputs, source_type and recency, are literally provenance fields — authority consumes them, it does not redefine them.

CONSTRUCT

04
INTUITION

An evidence bag, signed and timestamped at every hand-off

A chain of custody is what keeps a piece of physical evidence admissible: every person who touches the evidence bag signs it and timestamps the hand-off, so nobody can later claim the bag was tampered with, swapped, or altered without it showing up in the record. A document's lineage plays exactly that role for a fact moving through this pipeline — every stage that touches the record appends its own signed, timestamped event, so the fact arriving at the index can be traced back through every hand-off that produced it.

Analogy boundaryThe chain-of-custody analogy explains why an unbroken, ordered trail matters. It does not explain how to compute an ingestion lag in days, or what a non-monotonic timestamp actually looks like — Sections 05 and 09 give both formally.

06
MECHANISM

Two lanes: capture it live, or reconstruct it after the fact

CAPTURE-AT-EACH-STAGE LANE

Each of Concepts 02-07's own stages appends its own lineage event the moment it runs — the parser logs itself when it parses, the cleaner logs itself when it cleans. This is the default lane: accurate, but it depends on every stage actually being instrumented to log its own event.

BACKFILL LANE

When live capture was missed at some stage, a lineage chain can be best-effort reconstructed afterward from logs, timestamps on stored artifacts, or process metadata. This is a real fallback, not a preferred path — it is vulnerable to exactly the gaps Section 16 diagnoses.

09
MATHEMATICS

Lag, span, and the monotonicity a valid chain must satisfy

ingestion_lag = ingested_at − source_last_modified_at
total_lineage_span = final_stage_timestamp − source_created_at

A lineage chain L(doc_id) = (e1, …, en) is only valid if its timestamps are monotonic: t(e1) ≤ t(e2) ≤ … ≤ t(en). This is a new formal point this concept adds on top of Concept 07's own math — Concept 07 needed exactly two ordered timestamps per version; this concept needs an entire chain to hold that same ordering property across every stage, not just two. Any inversion — a later stage's timestamp landing before an earlier stage's — is itself a lineage-integrity error, not a value judgment about the data (Section 16 diagnoses exactly this failure mode).

ComputationFormulaResult
ingestion_lag(v1)2024-11-02 − 2024-10-285 days
ingestion_lag(v2)2025-06-18 − 2025-06-108 days
ingested_at(v1) → ingested_at(v2)2025-06-18 − 2024-11-02228 days — cross-checks Concept 07's published v1 lifespan exactly

Every figure here was computed once via an executed Node.js script (plain UTC Date arithmetic, millisecond difference divided by 86400000) and independently reproduced via an executed Python script (datetime.date subtraction, run through the py launcher). Both printed identical results for all five numbers, and the 228-day and 431-day figures are not fresh assertions — they are the same two numbers Concept 07 already published, arrived at from the lineage side instead of the versioning side, a genuine cross-concept consistency check rather than a coincidence.

11
CONCRETE USE

Two clean fits, a boundary case, a mistake

CLEAN FIT

A regulatory audit needing "who said this and when"

Provenance and timestamps together answer exactly that question, tying directly into Module 20's "Auditability, governance and compliance," named forward only.

CLEAN FIT

Debugging a bad chunk by walking its lineage back

A malformed value can be traced to the exact stage — parse, clean, or dedup — whose lineage event's timestamp brackets when the defect appeared.

BOUNDARY CASE

A record ingested via an aggregator that scraped an original source

Two provenance layers exist at once: the aggregator's own retrieval timestamp, and the original author's timestamp underneath it. Losing track of which is which is a real failure mode, covered in Section 16.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Treating ingested_at as the fact's real-world creation time

ingested_at only says when the pipeline noticed. source_created_at and source_last_modified_at, not ingested_at, describe when the fact actually came into being or last changed at the source.

12
CODE

A lineage chain with a monotonicity guard, from scratch

PYTHON · NO LIBRARIES BEYOND DATETIME 
from datetime import date

def append_event(chain, stage, concept_ref, day):
    if chain and day < chain[-1]["day"]:
        raise AssertionError(
            f'non-monotonic lineage: "{stage}" at {day} '
            f'precedes "{chain[-1]["stage"]}" at {chain[-1]["day"]}'
        )
    chain.append({"stage": stage, "concept_ref": concept_ref, "day": day})

def ingestion_lag(source_last_modified, ingested):
    return (ingested - source_last_modified).days

def total_lineage_span(source_created, final_stage_day):
    return (final_stage_day - source_created).days

chain = []
append_event(chain, "Source",             None,         date(2024, 10, 28))
append_event(chain, "Parse",               "Concept 02", date(2025, 6, 18))
append_event(chain, "Clean",               "Concept 05", date(2025, 6, 18))
append_event(chain, "Dedup/Canonicalize",  "Concept 06", date(2025, 6, 18))
append_event(chain, "Version",             "Concept 07", date(2025, 6, 18))

lag_v2 = ingestion_lag(date(2025, 6, 10), date(2025, 6, 18))
span = total_lineage_span(date(2024, 10, 28), date(2025, 6, 18))
assert lag_v2 == 8
assert span == 233

try:
    append_event(chain, "Broken stage", "n/a", date(2025, 1, 1))
except AssertionError as e:
    print("caught expected error:", e)

ECOSYSTEM

The same mechanism, in real standards and tools

The W3C PROV Model is the formal, real standard for representing provenance: it defines entities (the facts and records themselves), activities (the transformation steps that acted on them), and agents (who or what was responsible) — the same three ideas this concept's fields cover informally as source_id/lineage[]/author. OpenLineage is an open industry standard and toolset built specifically to auto-capture pipeline lineage as jobs run, the production parallel to Section 06's capture-at-each-stage lane rather than the backfill lane.

15
CONTRAST

Live-captured lineage vs. backfilled lineage

ApproachStrengthWeakness
Live-captured (Section 06's default lane)Accurate — every stage's own event is recorded the moment it happensRequires every stage to actually be instrumented to log itself; a missed instrumentation point leaves a real gap
Backfilled / reconstructed (Section 06's fallback lane)Works retroactively, even for records ingested before instrumentation existedVulnerable to the exact gaps Section 16 diagnoses — logs can be incomplete, timestamps can be approximate

Decision framework: default to live capture whenever a stage is still being built or maintained — instrument it once, and every future record benefits. Reach for backfill only for records that already passed through before instrumentation existed, and treat its output as best-effort, not authoritative.

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

MISSING TIMESTAMP

One stage in the chain never logged an event

Breaks the lag math directly — ingestion_lag and total_lineage_span both need every timestamp they reference to actually exist.

CLOCK SKEW

Cross-service clocks disagree, scoped to lineage timestamps specifically

Distinct from Concept 07's version-timestamp skew — here the risk is that two different SERVICES (the source system, the ingestion pipeline) disagree on the current time, corrupting ingestion_lag itself rather than an as-of query.

PROVENANCE DROPPED AT A PROXY

An aggregator or proxy strips the original source's own fields

The exact boundary case from Section 11 — losing track of which provenance layer (aggregator vs. original author) a fact actually belongs to.

19
RECALL

Knowledge check

Why can't provenance alone answer the question authority answers?
Provenance only supplies the raw facts — source_type, recency. Authority is the separate ranking rule, auth(s) = (type_rank(source_type), −recency), that decides how those facts should be compared across competing sources.
Why must a valid lineage chain's timestamps be monotonic?
Each event represents a real stage a record physically passed through, in order. A later stage's timestamp landing before an earlier stage's is not a data quirk — it means the recorded trail contradicts the order the pipeline actually ran in, which is itself an integrity error.
Why do two documents ingested through the same pipeline end up with lineage chains of different lengths?
Not every stage runs for every document — OCR (Concept 04) only runs for sources that need it, like scanned PDFs. A born-digital source can skip straight from parsing to cleaning, producing a genuinely shorter chain.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

How does provenance relate to a source-authority ranking system?
Provenance captures the raw facts about a source — its type, its recency. Authority is a separate ranking formula built on top of those facts. Design the two as producer and consumer, never as the same rule duplicated twice.
What does the chain-of-custody analogy get right, and where does it stop being useful?
It correctly conveys why an unbroken, ordered, signed trail matters for trust. It says nothing about how to compute a numeric lag or detect a broken (non-monotonic) chain programmatically — that requires the formal definitions and code, not the analogy alone.
Why would two documents from the same pipeline have lineage chains of different lengths?
Some stages are conditional on source type — OCR only runs for sources without a native text layer. A chain's length is a function of what that specific document actually needed, not a fixed pipeline constant.
How would you design a query to find documents whose ingestion lag exceeds a freshness budget?
Store ingested_at and source_last_modified_at per document, compute the difference per row, and filter for anything past the budget threshold — exactly the computation Section 14's lab performs live, generalized from one document to a whole corpus.
