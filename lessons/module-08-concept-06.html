MODULE 08 · CONCEPT 06

One sequence, many tokens.
One vector, by choice.

Concept 02's own pipeline called a function named pool_mean() as its fourth stage but never showed what it actually computed, or said it was one choice among real alternatives. This concept names three real pooling strategies -- mean pooling (average every token), CLS-token pooling (use one dedicated trained position), and last-token pooling (use the final position, valid only under causal attention) -- and computes, for the first time, what mean pooling actually produced on Concept 02's own four contextualized vectors for "The order shipped late," against what the other two strategies would have produced on the identical input. It also proves a fact Module 04 Concept 03 only flagged as a risk: once a vector is L2-normalized to unit length, a plain dot product already equals cosine similarity -- no separate division needed. 01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Three real strategies, one deliberate choice

THIS CONCEPT

Pooling collapses a SEQUENCE of contextualized token vectors into ONE fixed-size vector. Three real named strategies: mean pooling (average every token), CLS-token pooling (one dedicated trained position), last-token pooling (the final position -- only valid under causal/decoder-only attention).

NOT THIS CONCEPT

Anisotropy and embedding-space pathologies (Concept 07); embedding-model instructions and query/document prefixes (Concept 08) -- both named forward only.

First memory: mean averages everyone; CLS lets one trained spokesperson speak for the group; last-token lets whoever spoke last, having heard everyone else, give the final word.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

A named stage that was never actually shown

Concept 02's own pipeline called pool_mean() as its fourth stage but never showed what it computed or said why THAT choice over any other. Every concept since (03-05) silently assumed "the one final vector" already existed. The averaging arithmetic itself isn't new -- Module 07 already used it twice -- what's new here is naming it as one deliberate choice among real alternatives.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

What "pool" actually produced, for the first time

Reusing Concept 02's own four contextualized vectors for "The order shipped late": the=(1.1029,1.1812), order=(1.7468,1.2292), shipped=(1.6010,1.7274), late=(1.0859,1.8722). Mean pooling actually outputs (1.3842, 1.5025). First-token pooling (an illustrative CLS stand-in only -- this toy model has no real trained CLS position) would output "the" unchanged: (1.1029, 1.1812). Last-token pooling would output "late" unchanged: (1.0859, 1.8722).

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Pooling changes WHICH information survives, not the dimension

PairingThe real difference
Pooling vs. embedding dimensionPooling collapses the SEQUENCE/token axis; dimension is fixed by the encoder's own hidden size, untouched by which pooling strategy runs
Pooling vs. normalizationTwo separate, sequential steps -- pool first (many vectors → one), normalize after (rescale that one vector to unit length)
Last-token pooling vs. mean/CLS poolingRequires causal/decoder-only attention (only the final position saw everything); mean/CLS require bidirectional encoders
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"CLS-token pooling always works well."

False without dedicated training -- Sentence-BERT's own ablation: mean beats CLS by 0.82 points and max by 17.52 points on STS-benchmark.

"Bigger embedding dimension is free, or always better."

False -- storage and compute cost scale linearly with it; OpenAI's own docs describe shortening it as "trading off some accuracy."

"Pooling changes the vector's dimension."

False -- dimension is fixed by the encoder's hidden size; pooling only collapses the token axis.

INTUITION

How a group's opinions become one verdict

Average everyone's opinion (mean). Let one designated, specially-trained spokesperson speak for the group (CLS). Let whoever spoke last, having heard everyone else, give the final word (last-token -- valid only when speaking order actually restricts who heard what).

09
MATHEMATICS

Why normalizing first makes dot product equal cosine

mean = (1/N)·Σvᵢ — cited from Module 07 Concept 01, reused
For unit vectors u,v (‖u‖=‖v‖=1): cosine(u,v) = dot(u,v)/(‖u‖·‖v‖) = dot(u,v)/(1·1) = dot(u,v)

Composing Module 04 Concept 02's normalization with Module 04 Concept 03's cosine/dot-product formula -- not re-deriving either. Last-token pooling requires causal attention specifically because only the final position has attended to the whole sequence; under bidirectional attention every position already sees everything, so picking "last" would be arbitrary.

Honesty guard: this 2D toy example only demonstrates the arithmetic of each strategy; it understates real-world divergence. The real production stakes are Sentence-BERT's own Table 6 ablation on STS-benchmark: mean pooling (87.44 Spearman) beats CLS-token pooling (86.62) by 0.82 points and max pooling (69.92) by 17.52 points, specifically under the semantic-similarity objective. This toy model's "FIRST" is NOT a real trained CLS vector -- only an illustration of the selection rule.

Part 2 -- normalization makes dot product equal cosine. m_hat = mean/‖mean‖ = (0.677547, 0.735480), ‖m_hat‖=1.000000. late_hat = late/‖late‖ = (0.501726, 0.865026), ‖late_hat‖=1.000000. dot(m_hat, late_hat) = 0.976152 = cosine(mean, late) = 0.976152 -- identical to 6 decimal places, no division step in the dot product. The same equivalence holds for FIRST vs. LATE (dot of normalized = 0.974674 = cosine = 0.974674).

Part 3 -- what embedding dimension trades off (storage at float32 = 4 bytes/dimension, for a corpus of 1,000,000 chunks, reusing Module 04 Concept 09's O(n×d) formula): 384 dim (all-MiniLM-L6-v2) → 1.536 GB. 768 dim (BERT-base) → 3.072 GB. 1536 dim (OpenAI text-embedding-3-small/ada-002) → 6.144 GB. 3072 dim (OpenAI text-embedding-3-large, native) → 12.288 GB. Pooling collapses the sequence axis, not the feature axis -- output dimension equals the encoder's own hidden size regardless of pooling strategy. OpenAI's separate `dimensions` API parameter truncates the already-pooled vector afterward -- a distinct, later knob, not a fourth pooling strategy.

Verification method: every number above was computed twice, independently, with no shared code -- once in Node.js and once in Python -- and matched exactly on every integer/byte/GB figure and to 6 decimal places on every cosine/Euclidean/normalized-vector figure. The four base contextualized vectors are Concept 02's own already-verified numbers, reused unchanged.

CODE

Pooling and normalization, from scratch

PYTHON · NO LIBRARIES 
import math

def mean_pool(vectors):
    n = len(vectors)
    return (sum(v[0] for v in vectors) / n, sum(v[1] for v in vectors) / n)

def cls_pool(vectors):
    # Illustrative stand-in for a dedicated, trained CLS position.
    return vectors[0]

def last_token_pool(vectors):
    # Valid only under causal/decoder-only attention.
    return vectors[-1]

def norm(v):
    return math.sqrt(v[0]**2 + v[1]**2)

def l2_normalize(v):
    n = norm(v)
    return (v[0] / n, v[1] / n)

def dot(u, v):
    return u[0]*v[0] + u[1]*v[1]

the, order, shipped, late = (1.1029,1.1812), (1.7468,1.2292), (1.6010,1.7274), (1.0859,1.8722)
mean = mean_pool([the, order, shipped, late])
assert round(mean[0], 4) == 1.3842 and round(mean[1], 4) == 1.5025

m_hat, late_hat = l2_normalize(mean), l2_normalize(late)
assert round(norm(m_hat), 6) == 1.0
assert round(dot(m_hat, late_hat), 6) == 0.976152

REALITY

Production implications

  • Storage/index memory cost scales linearly with dimension (cite Module 04 Concept 09's O(n×d)); every downstream similarity computation's compute cost also scales with dimension.
  • Pooling strategy is baked into a pretrained model's own training and cannot be swapped post-hoc without a real quality hit (cite the ablation again for the size of that hit).
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

Define mean, CLS-token and last-token pooling.
Mean: average every contextualized token vector. CLS-token: use one dedicated, trained position's vector. Last-token: use the final position's vector, valid only under causal attention.
Why does last-token pooling need causal attention specifically?
Only the final position has attended to the whole sequence under causal masking. Under bidirectional attention every position already sees everything, so picking "last" specifically would be arbitrary.
Why does normalizing first make dot product equal cosine similarity?
Cosine similarity is dot(u,v)/(‖u‖·‖v‖). If both vectors already have unit length (‖u‖=‖v‖=1), the denominator is 1, so cosine collapses to the plain dot product.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: why does pooling choice actually matter?
Different strategies genuinely produce different vectors from the same input -- Section 10's own table shows first-token and last-token diverging by Euclidean 0.691209, and Sentence-BERT's real ablation shows a 0.82-to-17.52-point STS-benchmark gap depending on the strategy.
Mathematical: prove dot equals cosine for unit vectors.
cosine(u,v) = dot(u,v)/(‖u‖·‖v‖). If ‖u‖=‖v‖=1, the denominator is 1, so cosine(u,v) = dot(u,v) exactly.
Coding: implement mean/CLS/last-token pooling plus L2 normalization.
Section 12 gives the full from-scratch implementation, with asserts reproducing this concept's own Section 10 numbers.
Debugging: a retriever's STS-style eval scores are mysteriously low. What should you check?
Whether CLS-token pooling is being used on an encoder that was never fine-tuned with a dedicated CLS training objective -- an untuned CLS vector performs far worse than mean pooling on semantic-similarity tasks.
System design: how would you choose an embedding model's dimension for a fixed storage budget?
Compute the storage cost at corpus scale (bytes/vector × corpus size, Module 04 Concept 09's O(n×d)) for each candidate dimension, then pick the largest dimension that fits the budget -- checking whether a smaller dimension's real capacity loss (cite the ablation-style evidence for the specific model family) is acceptable for the task.
