MODULE 08 · CONCEPT 10

Four filters.
One real pick.

Concepts 06-09 each named a real property of an embedding model in isolation -- dimension and storage cost, anisotropy, instruction prefixes, and the three model categories (general-purpose, multilingual, domain-specific). None of them actually chose a model for a real corpus. This concept gives the missing process: a four-step funnel -- filter by language mix, filter by domain, filter by storage budget, then rank whatever survives on MTEB, the real 58-dataset, 112-language benchmark practitioners actually use. A legal-tech company's bilingual, jargon-dense, 2,000,000-chunk corpus with a 10 GB storage cap eliminates two of four candidate dimensions outright -- 1536 dimensions costs 12.288 GB, 3072 costs 24.576 GB, both over budget -- leaving only 384 (3.072 GB) and 768 (6.144 GB) to rank on the real leaderboard.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

A four-step funnel, not four separate facts

1 · LANGUAGE FILTER

The corpus's language mix rules whole categories in or out -- reuses Concept 09's own general/multilingual/domain-specific names.

2 · DOMAIN FILTER

Jargon density narrows further toward a domain-tuned model, or an honest compromise when no perfect match exists.

3 · BUDGET FILTER

Concept 06's storage formula, scaled to the corpus's real chunk count, eliminates dimensions that simply do not fit.

4 · BENCHMARK RANK

MTEB ranks whatever survives the first three filters -- the real, cited, publicly browsable leaderboard, not a guess.

First memory: naming a property (Concepts 06-09) is not the same as choosing a model for a real corpus -- this funnel is the missing step.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Nobody has actually chosen a model yet

Every property named so far -- pooling and dimension, anisotropy, prefixes, the three model categories -- needs to be combined into one concrete decision before any real embedding happens. A team cannot ship with "we know embedding models have dimensions" as an answer; they need the actual dimension, for their actual corpus, under their actual budget.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

A bilingual, jargon-dense, 2,000,000-chunk corpus with a 10 GB cap

A legal-tech company's support corpus is bilingual (English + Spanish), dense with legal jargon, has 2,000,000 chunks, and has a hard 10 GB storage cap. Step 1 (language): bilingual rules out a purely monolingual model -- Concept 09's "multilingual" category. Step 2 (domain): legal jargon points further toward "domain-specific," or the best available compromise. Step 3 (budget): at float32 storage (4 bytes/dimension) across 2,000,000 chunks, 384 dim costs 3.072 GB, 768 dim costs 6.144 GB -- both fit. 1536 dim costs 12.288 GB and 3072 dim costs 24.576 GB -- both exceed the budget and are eliminated. Step 4 (rank): the two survivors get ranked on MTEB's real leaderboard, filtered to English/Spanish and retrieval tasks -- no score is invented here; that ranking happens on the live leaderboard, not in this lesson.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

The four filters, named plainly

Language filter narrows the category (general-purpose / multilingual / domain-specific). Domain filter narrows further, or names the honest compromise when no single model covers both language and domain perfectly. Budget filter applies Concept 06's byte-cost formula at the corpus's real chunk count and removes any dimension that would not fit. Benchmark rank takes whatever survives the first three filters and orders it on MTEB, filtered to the corpus's actual languages and task type.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"The top MTEB model is always the right pick."

False -- ignores budget and latency entirely, and can produce an index too large to afford.

"Bigger dimension is always better."

False -- reuses Concept 06's own correction; bigger dimension costs more storage and compute for a capacity gain that may not matter.

"One model can always cover every language and domain at once."

False -- Step 2's honest compromise; a perfectly matched multilingual-and-domain-tuned model may simply not exist yet.

INTUITION

A shopper with a language requirement, a specialty requirement and a fixed budget

A shopper narrows a catalog before checking reviews: first by which language the product ships in, then by whether it fits a specialty need, then by whether it fits the budget at all -- only after all three filters does checking reviews (MTEB) on what's left make sense. Checking reviews first, on the whole catalog, wastes time on options that were never affordable or never in the right language to begin with.

MECHANISM

The four-step funnel, walked in order

Step 1, language: the corpus's real language mix rules whole categories out -- a bilingual corpus rules out a purely monolingual model, reusing Concept 09's own category names without re-arguing why (Concept 09 already showed the negative-cosine consequence of skipping this filter). Step 2, domain: jargon density narrows further toward a domain-specific candidate, or names the honest compromise when no perfectly matched multilingual-and-domain-tuned model exists. Step 3, budget: Concept 06's exact per-dimension byte cost, bytes = n × d × 4, applied at the corpus's real chunk count, eliminates any dimension that would not fit -- this step needs no benchmark data at all, only arithmetic. Step 4, benchmark rank: MTEB ranks whatever survives Steps 1-3, filtered to the corpus's actual languages and task type, and the practitioner picks the higher-scoring survivor.

10
WORKED EXAMPLE

The full legal-tech funnel, four steps

Scenario. A legal-tech company's customer-support corpus is bilingual (English + Spanish), dense with legal jargon, has 2,000,000 chunks, and has a hard storage budget cap of 10 GB for the embedding index (float32 storage, 4 bytes/dimension).

Step 1 -- language filter. Bilingual (English + Spanish) requires a model from Concept 09's "multilingual" category -- a general English-only or purely monolingual-domain model is eliminated here, reusing Concept 09's own category names, not re-arguing why.

Step 2 -- domain filter. Legal jargon density additionally points toward Concept 09's "domain-specific" category, or at minimum a candidate that scores well on MTEB's retrieval-style tasks for legal or general text. A perfectly matched multilingual-legal-tuned model may not exist -- the framework's honest output here is "narrow toward the best available compromise," not a guaranteed perfect match.

Step 3 -- storage/budget filter. Applying Concept 06's exact per-dimension byte cost (bytes = n × d × 4) to n = 2,000,000 chunks: 384 dim → 2,000,000 × 384 × 4 = 3,072,000,000 bytes = 3.072 GB (fits). 768 dim → 2,000,000 × 768 × 4 = 6,144,000,000 bytes = 6.144 GB (fits). 1536 dim → 2,000,000 × 1536 × 4 = 12,288,000,000 bytes = 12.288 GB (exceeds -- eliminated). 3072 dim → 2,000,000 × 3072 × 4 = 24,576,000,000 bytes = 24.576 GB (exceeds -- eliminated). Every one of these four values is exactly double Concept 06's own 1,000,000-chunk figures (1.536 / 3.072 / 6.144 / 12.288 GB), the expected, checkable consequence of doubling n in a formula linear in n.

Step 4 -- rank the survivors. 384 dim and 768 dim rank on MTEB's real, cited leaderboard, filtered to English, Spanish, and retrieval / bitext-mining tasks. No specific current MTEB score is asserted for any named model here -- leaderboard standings change over time as new models are submitted, and a stale point-in-time claim would be worse than no claim. The framework's contribution is the four-step procedure and the fully computed budget filter, not a fabricated leaderboard score.

Verification method: the four budget values were computed via a Node.js loop over the four dimensions, and independently re-computed via a second, structurally different script writing each value as a separate hand-written statement and asserting equality with exactly double Concept 06's cached 1,000,000-chunk figures -- both passes matched on every value.

CONCRETE USE

A clean fit, a boundary case, and a mistake

CLEAN FIT

The worked scenario itself

Bilingual, jargon-dense, 2,000,000 chunks, 10 GB cap -- all four filters engage and narrow to two survivors.

BOUNDARY CASE

A corpus with no budget constraint at all

The funnel collapses to just language filter → domain filter → MTEB rank -- Step 3 simply has nothing to eliminate.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Picking the single highest MTEB score, ignoring the budget filter

Produces an index too large to actually afford -- the score was never checked against the constraint that mattered most.

CODE

The funnel, from scratch

PYTHON · REUSES CONCEPT 06'S BYTE-COST ARITHMETIC UNCHANGED 
def storage_gb(n_chunks, dim):
    return (n_chunks * dim * 4) / 1e9

def funnel(languages, domain, budget_gb, n_chunks):
    dims = [384, 768, 1536, 3072]
    survivors = [d for d in dims if storage_gb(n_chunks, d) <= budget_gb]
    multilingual = languages > 1
    specialized = domain == "specialized"
    if multilingual and specialized:
        category = ("Multilingual + domain-specific -- rare combination, "
                     "check MTEB's task-specific leaderboard filters or "
                     "plan a routed/ensemble approach")
    elif multilingual:
        category = "Multilingual"
    elif specialized:
        category = "Domain-specific"
    else:
        category = "General-purpose"
    return category, survivors

category, survivors = funnel(languages=2, domain="specialized",
                              budget_gb=10, n_chunks=2_000_000)

assert round(storage_gb(2_000_000, 384), 3) == 3.072
assert round(storage_gb(2_000_000, 768), 3) == 6.144
assert round(storage_gb(2_000_000, 1536), 3) == 12.288
assert round(storage_gb(2_000_000, 3072), 3) == 24.576
assert survivors == [384, 768]
assert category.startswith("Multilingual + domain-specific")

ECOSYSTEM

MTEB, plus the models it ranks

MTEB (Muennighoff, Tazi, Magne & Reimers) spans 8 embedding tasks covering 58 datasets and 112 languages, benchmarking 33 models at publication -- open-source, with a public leaderboard. The live MTEB leaderboard is the real tool a practitioner filters by language and task in Step 4, not a number this lesson could hardcode. The candidates being ranked are the same models named by Concepts 06 and 09: all-MiniLM-L6-v2, BERT-base, and OpenAI's text-embedding-3-small/large anchor the 384/768/1536/3072-dimension rows; LaBSE, BGE-M3, MedCPT and CodeRetriever anchor the multilingual and domain-specific categories Step 1 and Step 2 filter toward.

FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open the worked solution
Step 1: a single language (English) does not require the "multilingual" category. Step 2: medical jargon density points toward "domain-specific" (a model like MedCPT). Step 3: at 500,000 chunks, storage cost is exactly a quarter of the lesson's own 2,000,000-chunk figures -- 384 dim = 0.768 GB, 768 dim = 1.536 GB, 1536 dim = 3.072 GB, 3072 dim = 6.144 GB. Against a 2 GB budget, 384 dim and 768 dim survive; 1536 dim and 3072 dim are eliminated. Step 4 would then rank those two survivors on MTEB's biomedical-retrieval-filtered leaderboard.

Teach-back: why does a smaller corpus (500,000 vs. 2,000,000 chunks) let more dimensions survive the same-sized budget, without anything about the model itself changing?

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: why use MTEB instead of just trusting a vendor's marketing claim?
MTEB is a standardized, open, multi-task, multi-language benchmark with a public leaderboard -- it lets a practitioner compare candidates on the same tasks and datasets, rather than trusting a vendor's own selectively chosen numbers.
Mathematical: verify the budget filter arithmetic for n=2,000,000.
bytes = n × d × 4. For d=384: 2,000,000×384×4=3,072,000,000 bytes=3.072 GB. For d=768: 6,144,000,000 bytes=6.144 GB. For d=1536: 12,288,000,000 bytes=12.288 GB. For d=3072: 24,576,000,000 bytes=24.576 GB -- each exactly double Concept 06's own 1,000,000-chunk figures.
Coding: implement the funnel.
Section 12 gives the full from-scratch implementation -- a storage_gb() helper reusing Concept 06's arithmetic unchanged, and a funnel() function returning a category label and the list of budget-surviving dimensions.
Debugging: a chosen model scores well on MTEB overall but poorly for the actual corpus. What's the first thing to check?
Whether the corpus's actual language, domain and task type actually match what MTEB measured for that model -- an overall-strong score can still hide a weak result on the specific language/task slice that matters for this corpus.
System design: design a selection process for a corpus spanning three languages and two domains.
Run the same four-step funnel: Step 1 requires a multilingual category covering all three languages; Step 2 checks whether any single model is tuned for both domains, and if not, names that as the honest compromise (or considers a routed/ensemble approach per two domains); Step 3 applies the budget filter at the corpus's real chunk count; Step 4 ranks survivors on MTEB filtered to all three languages and the relevant task types.
