MODULE 05 · CONCEPT 09

It's not just whether —
it's where.

Concept 08 gave you precision@k, recall@k and F1@k — three numbers built entirely from an unordered top-k set, blind to whether a relevant document sits at rank 1 or rank k. This concept fixes exactly that blind spot with three rank-aware metrics: MRR looks at where the first relevant result lands, MAP averages precision at every relevant rank, and DCG — normalized into nDCG — discounts every relevant hit by a decreasing function of its position. None of these replace Concept 08's metrics; they're a complementary layer that finally lets position count.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Three metrics that finally care where, not just whether

CONCEPT 08 — SET-BASED

Precision@k and recall@k treat the top k as one unordered set. A relevant document at rank 1 and one at rank k score identically.

CONCEPT 09 — RANK-AWARE

MRR, MAP and DCG/nDCG all weight a relevant hit by where inside the top k it actually sits — three different ways of making position count.

First memory: precision/recall don't care if the one relevant hit is at rank 1 or rank 5 — these three metrics all do, in three different ways.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Nobody reads to the bottom of the results page

A real user — or an LLM's limited context window — only ever looks at the first few results. A relevant document buried at rank 20 is worth far less in practice than the same document at rank 1, even though Concept 08's metrics score them identically once both happen to land inside, or both happen to land outside, the same cutoff k. Something has to capture that difference. That's this concept's entire job.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Two queries, so "mean" actually means something

Query A reuses Concept 08's own eight-document list — relevant = positions {1, 2, 4, 7}. Query B is a new, shorter list: five documents, only position 3 is relevant.

QUERY A

First relevant hit at rank 1

Reciprocal rank RR_A = 1/1 = 1.000 — the very first result is already relevant.

QUERY B

First (and only) relevant hit at rank 3

Reciprocal rank RR_B = 1/3 = 0.333 — the one relevant document is buried two spots down.

MRR is the mean of the two: MRR = mean(1.000, 0.333) = 0.667. A single query's RR isn't a metric anyone reports on its own — MRR only means something averaged over more than one query, which is exactly why Query B exists here.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Three formulas, three different questions

MetricFormula (per query, then averaged)Question it answers
MRRRR = 1 / rank of first relevant hit; MRR = mean(RR) across queriesHow far down the list did I have to go before hitting anything relevant, on average?
MAPAP = mean of precision@rank at every relevant rank; MAP = mean(AP) across queriesOnce I found a relevant document, how clean was everything up to that point — averaged over every relevant hit, not just the first?
DCG → nDCGDCG@k = Σ 1/log2(rank+1) over relevant ranks; nDCG@k = DCG@k / IDCG@kHow much total, position-discounted credit did this ranking earn, compared to the best possible ordering for this exact query?

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Three distinctions worth keeping straight

TermIs NOTWhy it matters
MRRMAPMRR only ever looks at the first relevant hit and stops. MAP keeps going — it averages precision at every relevant rank, so a system that finds the first hit fast but buries the rest still gets penalized by MAP even though MRR wouldn't notice.
DCGnDCGDCG is unbounded and grows with list length and how many relevant documents exist. nDCG divides by that same query's best-possible DCG (IDCG), producing a 0-1 score that's actually comparable across different queries.
Binary relevance (all four metrics)Graded relevanceMRR and MAP need a strict relevant/not-relevant flag. DCG and nDCG extend naturally to graded judgments (0-3 star helpfulness, say) — where those grades actually come from is Concept 10's job, previewed here only.
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"MRR cares about every relevant document."

False — it only ever cares about the first one. A system that finds one relevant hit at rank 1 and then buries nine more relevant documents at rank 50 still scores a perfect MRR of 1.0.

"DCG alone is comparable across queries."

False — only nDCG is. Two queries with different numbers of relevant documents have different maximum-possible DCG (IDCG), so their raw DCG scores answer different questions even when the numbers look similar.

"Average precision averages over every rank in the list."

False — it only averages precision at the ranks where a relevant document actually sits, skipping every non-relevant rank entirely.

"These replace precision, recall and F1."

False — they're a complementary, position-sensitive layer on top. Concept 08's metrics are still reported; this concept adds to them, it doesn't retire them.

09 · WHAT YOU SHOULD UNDERSTAND

Readiness check before moving on

RR/MRR is first-hit-only

Reciprocal rank looks at the first relevant document and stops — nothing after it changes the score.

AP/MAP averages precision at every relevant rank, then across queries

AP is a within-query average over relevant ranks; MAP is the further average of AP across a set of queries.

DCG discounts by log-position; IDCG is the best-possible DCG for that query; nDCG is the ratio

Dividing by IDCG is what makes nDCG comparable across queries with different numbers of relevant documents — raw DCG alone is not.

This is a strict superset of Concept 08, not a replacement

Position now counts, but nothing that mattered before (whether a document is relevant at all) stops mattering.

04
INTUITION

How far down the results page, and how much of a ramp

MRR is "how far down the results page did you have to scroll before finding one real answer" — nothing else about the page matters once you've found it. MAP is "did you find all the good answers, not just the first one" — it keeps grading the page past that first hit. DCG's discount is a ramp, not a cliff: results near the top count close to full value, and that value decays smoothly the further down the list a relevant document sits, rather than mattering fully inside a cutoff and not at all outside it.

06
MECHANISM

Scan, weight, and — for DCG — normalize

Scan top-down to the first relevant rank — invert it. That's RR. Stop; nothing after this rank affects RR.
At every relevant rank, compute precision@that rank — average those within the query to get AP, then average AP across queries to get MAP.
Weight every relevant hit by 1/log2(rank+1) and sum — that's DCG@k, a single position-discounted score for this ranking.
Compute the same sum for the ideal ordering — that's IDCG@k, the best this exact query could possibly score.
Divide DCG@k by IDCG@k — that's nDCG@k, a 0-1 score that's finally comparable across different queries.

09
MATHEMATICS

Why log2(i+1), why IDCG is per-query, and why MRR/MAP need no normalizing

The discount uses log2(i+1) rather than log2(i) so that rank 1 never divides by log2(1)=0 — the +1 shift keeps every rank's denominator strictly positive. Because log2(i+1) is strictly increasing in i, its reciprocal 1/log2(i+1) is strictly decreasing, which is exactly the guarantee that an earlier rank always weighs at least as much as a later one, never less.

DCG@k = Σ_(i=1)^k rel_i / log2(i+1) — strictly decreasing weight as rank i grows

IDCG is query-specific: it depends on how many relevant documents (or, with graded relevance, how many high-grade documents) exist for that particular query, not on some fixed global constant. Two queries with different relevant-document counts have different best-possible DCG values, so their raw DCG scores are not answering the same question even at the same k. Dividing by each query's own IDCG is what makes nDCG, not raw DCG, the metric that's valid to compare across queries.

MRR and MAP need no equivalent normalizing step, because they're built from precision terms that are each already bounded to [0,1] by construction — a mean of numbers already in [0,1] stays in [0,1]. DCG has no such built-in ceiling, which is precisely why it alone needs the extra IDCG division nDCG supplies.

11
CONCRETE USE

Four cards: two clean fits, a boundary case, a mistake

CLEAN FIT

MRR for "did you mean" / first-correct-answer UX

When only the top hit matters to the user — a chatbot's single best answer, a spell-correction suggestion — MRR is the metric shaped exactly like that experience.

CLEAN FIT

nDCG for graded, search-result-page evaluation

Once relevance is a 0-3 star scale rather than a flat flag, nDCG is the metric built to use those grades directly, weighted by position.

BOUNDARY CASE

No relevant document appears anywhere in the list

MRR is 0 by convention in this case — a different edge case from Concept 08's, where recall was undefined because zero relevant documents existed for the query at all. Here relevant documents exist; the ranking just never surfaced one.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Comparing two queries' raw DCG@k scores directly

Valid only if both queries happen to have the same number of relevant documents. In general, only nDCG@k — normalized by each query's own IDCG — is safe to compare across queries.

CODE

RR, AP, DCG, IDCG and nDCG, from scratch

PYTHON · NO LIBRARIES 
import math

def rr(relevant_ranks, n):
    for i in range(1, n + 1):
        if i in relevant_ranks:
            return 1 / i
    return 0.0

def average_precision(relevant_ranks, n):
    if not relevant_ranks:
        return 0.0
    seen = 0
    total = 0.0
    for i in range(1, n + 1):
        if i in relevant_ranks:
            seen += 1
            total += seen / i
    return total / len(relevant_ranks)

def dcg_at_k(relevant_ranks, k):
    return sum(1 / math.log2(i + 1) for i in range(1, k + 1) if i in relevant_ranks)

def idcg_at_k(num_relevant, k):
    ideal_hits = min(num_relevant, k)
    return sum(1 / math.log2(i + 1) for i in range(1, ideal_hits + 1))

def ndcg_at_k(relevant_ranks, k):
    idcg = idcg_at_k(len(relevant_ranks), k)
    return dcg_at_k(relevant_ranks, k) / idcg if idcg > 0 else 0.0

relA, nA = {1, 2, 4, 7}, 8
relB, nB = {3}, 5

RR_A, RR_B = rr(relA, nA), rr(relB, nB)
MRR = (RR_A + RR_B) / 2
AP_A, AP_B = average_precision(relA, nA), average_precision(relB, nB)
MAP = (AP_A + AP_B) / 2
nDCG_8 = ndcg_at_k(relA, 8)

assert RR_A == 1.0 and round(RR_B, 3) == 0.333
assert round(MRR, 3) == 0.667
assert round(AP_A, 3) == 0.830
assert round(MAP, 3) == 0.582
assert round(nDCG_8, 3) == 0.935

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

APPLES-TO-ORANGES DCG

Reporting raw DCG across differently-sized query sets

Two queries with different numbers of relevant documents have different maximum-possible DCG. Comparing their raw DCG scores as if they meant the same thing is exactly the mistake nDCG exists to prevent.

MRR-ONLY TUNNEL VISION

Trusting MRR alone for a system that finds one hit fast but buries the rest

A system can score a perfect MRR while completely failing to surface most of its relevant documents. Pair MRR with MAP or nDCG before declaring a system good.

HARDCODED IDCG

Using a fixed IDCG instead of computing it per query

A real coding bug: IDCG depends on how many relevant documents that specific query actually has. Hardcoding it silently produces nDCG values that don't mean what they claim to.

REALITY

Production implications

  • Averaging MRR, MAP and nDCG over a real held-out set of queries, with proper significance testing, is Module 18's job — same as Concept 08's own metrics, this concept works one query at a time on purpose to make the mechanics visible.
  • nDCG@10 is the de facto standard cutoff in web-search-style evaluation and TREC tracks — worth knowing by name even before Module 18 covers the harness that computes it at scale.
  • MRR is popular for QA and chat "first correct answer" UX, while nDCG dominates ranked search-result-page evaluation with graded judgments — the choice tends to track the product surface, not just mathematical preference.
  • Collecting graded (not just binary) relevance judgments costs meaningfully more than binary ones — a real production trade-off, forward-referenced to Concept 10 without being taught here.
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

Why does MRR ignore every relevant hit after the first?
By definition, RR is 1 divided by the rank of the first relevant document — the moment that rank is found, the computation stops. Nothing about documents further down the list, relevant or not, changes the value.
Why isn't raw DCG comparable across two queries, while nDCG is?
DCG's maximum possible value (IDCG) depends on how many relevant documents that specific query has — a query with more relevant documents can reach a higher raw DCG purely from having more to work with. nDCG divides by each query's own IDCG, cancelling that difference out.
You compute nDCG@10 = 1.3 for a query. What does that reveal?
nDCG can never legitimately exceed 1.0, since DCG@k can never exceed IDCG@k by definition (IDCG is the best possible ordering). A value above 1.0 points to a bug — almost always an IDCG computed wrong or incomplete for that query.

Concept card

MRR, MAP, DCG and nDCG

  • RR/MRR: 1/rank of the first relevant hit, then averaged — first-hit only
  • AP/MAP: mean of precision@rank at every relevant rank, then averaged — all hits
  • DCG@k: Σ 1/log2(rank+1) over relevant ranks — a position-discounted sum
  • nDCG@k: DCG@k / IDCG@k — the cross-query-comparable, normalized version

Memorable example

Query A (rank 1 hit) and Query B (rank 3 hit): MRR=0.667, MAP=0.582. Query A alone at k=8: nDCG@8=0.935 — three numbers, three different ways of rewarding position.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Why might a chatbot team prefer MRR over nDCG for evaluating retrieval?
If the product only ever shows the user one answer, or only the top hit ever reaches the LLM's prompt, MRR is shaped exactly like that experience — it measures precisely the thing the user notices. nDCG would credit a system for surfacing relevant documents the user never actually sees.
Explain why the DCG discount uses log2(rank+1) rather than log2(rank).
log2(1) is 0, which would make rank 1's term divide by zero. Shifting to log2(rank+1) keeps every rank's denominator strictly positive while preserving the same strictly-decreasing discount shape.
You compute nDCG@10 greater than 1.0 for a query — what's the most likely bug?
An incorrect or incomplete IDCG@10 — most often computed from the wrong number of relevant documents for that query, or hardcoded rather than derived from that query's own relevance judgments. nDCG can never legitimately exceed 1.0.
Design question: how would you choose between reporting MRR, MAP or nDCG for a given product surface?
Match the metric to what the product actually shows the user. If only the single best answer matters, MRR. If the product needs to surface every relevant result in a reasonable order, MAP. If relevance itself is graded rather than binary, nDCG — it's the only one of the three built to use grades directly.
