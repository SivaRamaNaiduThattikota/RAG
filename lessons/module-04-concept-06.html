MODULE 04 · CONCEPT 06

Softmax, temperature
and logarithms.

Concept 05 defined what a discrete probability distribution actually is, starting from outcomes and counts, with no vectors involved. This concept is where that thread meets Concepts 01-04's raw scores: softmax takes any list of real-valued numbers — similarity scores, negative distances, a model's raw logits — and turns them into a genuine probability distribution that satisfies Concept 05's sum-to-1 rule. Temperature, a single positive number dividing every score before exponentiating, controls how sharply softmax favors the largest one. Logarithms turn up for two separate reasons: they keep the arithmetic numerically stable when scores get large, and — because log is monotonically increasing — taking a log never changes which score was largest, a fact Concept 07's cross-entropy and Concept 08's ranking objectives both lean on directly.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Softmax: exponentiate every score, then divide by the total

SOFTMAX

Given scores z₁, …, zₙ: softmax(zᵢ) = exp(zᵢ) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ). Every output is positive, and they all add up to exactly 1 — Concept 05's distribution requirement, satisfied by construction.

TEMPERATURE, T

Divide every score by T before exponentiating: softmax(zᵢ/T). Small T (below 1) sharpens the distribution toward the largest score; large T (above 1) flattens it toward uniform.

WHY EXPONENTIATE AT ALL

Raw scores can be negative or zero, which a probability can never be. exp(x) is positive for every real x, so exponentiating first guarantees every output clears that bar before the division even happens.

LOGARITHMS, PREVIEWED

Taking a log undoes an exponential and never changes which score was largest — log is monotonically increasing. Both facts get used directly in Section 09's log-sum-exp trick and in Concept 07's cross-entropy.

First memory: softmax(zᵢ) = exp(zᵢ/T) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ/T) — always positive, always sums to 1, and temperature T controls how sharply the largest score dominates the rest.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Raw scores fail Concept 05's own requirement

A cosine similarity can be negative. A raw model logit can be any real number at all, positive or negative, with no upper bound. Neither one sums to 1 across a set of candidates, and neither is guaranteed to be non-negative. Concept 05 was explicit about what a distribution requires; softmax is the specific, differentiable function that takes any such list and produces something that actually qualifies — without throwing away the relative order of the original scores.

WITHOUT SOFTMAX

Three candidate documents score 2.0, 1.0, and 0.1 against a query. Read directly, there's no sense in which these "add up to 1," and 2.0 isn't a probability of anything.

WITH SOFTMAX

The same three scores become a genuine distribution — about 0.66, 0.24, and 0.10 at temperature 1 (computed in b03) — that can be used directly as a probability of relevance, or as the input to a loss function (Concept 07).

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Three candidate documents, one query, one temperature at a time

A query is scored against three retrieved candidates, giving raw similarity scores z = [2.0, 1.0, 0.1]. At temperature T = 1, softmax turns these into a distribution as follows.

EXPONENTIATE EACH SCORE

exp(2.0) ≈ 7.389, exp(1.0) ≈ 2.718, exp(0.1) ≈ 1.105.

SUM THE EXPONENTIALS

7.389 + 2.718 + 1.105 ≈ 11.213.

DIVIDE EACH BY THE SUM

7.389/11.213 ≈ 0.659, 2.718/11.213 ≈ 0.242, 1.105/11.213 ≈ 0.099.

CHECK: DO THEY SUM TO 1?

0.659 + 0.242 + 0.099 = 1.000 — exactly, up to rounding. Concept 05's requirement, satisfied.

The highest-scoring candidate keeps the largest share of the probability mass, but the other two are not zeroed out — softmax is a soft, differentiable version of picking the single best candidate, not a hard cutoff. Temperature (b04, Section 07) controls exactly how soft.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Two steps, applied to every score in the list

STEP 1 — SCALE

Divide by temperature

Every score zᵢ becomes zᵢ/T first. This step alone does nothing to the ranking — dividing by a positive number never swaps which score is largest.

STEP 2 — EXPONENTIATE

Apply exp to every scaled score

exp is strictly increasing, so the largest scaled score still produces the largest exponential — order preserved again.

STEP 3 — NORMALIZE

Divide each exponential by their sum

Every term is divided by the same positive number, so relative order is preserved a third time — this is why softmax never re-ranks its inputs (Section 09).

RESULT

A genuine distribution, same ranking

The output satisfies Concept 05's sum-to-1 rule, and sorting by softmax output gives the identical order as sorting by the original raw scores.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"Softmax changes which candidate had the best score."

It never does, for any temperature T > 0 — softmax only reshapes how much probability mass separates the candidates, not their order (Section 09).

"A higher temperature makes softmax more confident."

Backwards — b03's own numbers show the top share falling from 0.864 at T=0.5 to 0.502 at T=2. Lower temperature sharpens; higher temperature flattens toward uniform.

"Temperature divides the final probabilities, not the raw scores."

It divides the scores before exponentiating, not after. Dividing the final probabilities by T would not even produce numbers that still sum to 1.

"Softmax output can be read directly as a percentage confidence that generalizes to new data."

It's a valid probability distribution over the given candidates only — whether it's well calibrated against real-world outcomes is a separate question, deferred to Module 18 (forward reference).

INTUITION

A dimmer switch on how confidently one candidate wins

Picture the three retrieval scores from b03 as three runners crossing a finish line at slightly different times. Argmax only ever announces the winner and forgets the other two. Softmax instead hands out a full set of "how likely is each one to actually be the right answer" shares, all adding to 1, and temperature is a dimmer switch on how strongly the winner's share dominates: turn it down and the winner takes almost everything, turn it up and the three shares drift toward an even split.

Analogy boundaryThe runners only illustrate degree of dominance, not the exact arithmetic — the exact shares at any temperature come from Section 09's formula, computed precisely, not estimated from a picture.

MECHANISM

Tracing softmax at T=1 and T=0.5, step by step

Start with raw scores — z = [2.0, 1.0, 0.1], T = 1.

09
MATHEMATICS

Ranking preservation and the log-sum-exp trick

Claim 1 — softmax never changes the ranking. For any T > 0 and any two scores zᵢ > zⱼ:

zᵢ > zⱼ ⇒ zᵢ/T > zⱼ/T (T > 0, dividing preserves order) ⇒ exp(zᵢ/T) > exp(zⱼ/T) (exp is strictly increasing) ⇒ exp(zᵢ/T)/S > exp(zⱼ/T)/S (S = Σₖ exp(zₖ/T) > 0, same S for every term) ⇒ softmax_T(zᵢ) > softmax_T(zⱼ)

Every arrow is a known monotonic step applied to both sides at once, so the final inequality is forced — softmax_T can never flip which of two scores is larger, for any positive temperature. This is exactly why Section 07's curve, and b03's bar chart, never change which candidate is on top, no matter where temperature is set.

Claim 2 — the log-sum-exp trick keeps the normalizing sum computable. Directly computing Σⱼ exp(zⱼ) overflows in floating-point once any zⱼ gets even moderately large (exp(1000), for instance, is far beyond what a 64-bit float can represent). Subtracting the maximum score first fixes this without changing the answer:

log Σⱼ exp(zⱼ) = m + log Σⱼ exp(zⱼ − m), m = max(z) softmax(zᵢ) = exp(zᵢ − m) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ − m)

Subtracting m makes the largest exponent exactly 0 (exp(0) = 1) and every other exponent negative, so every term in the new sum lies in (0, 1] — no overflow is possible, and the algebra guarantees the result is identical to the unshifted version. This is the same identity every production softmax and cross-entropy implementation runs internally (Section 13).

11
CONCRETE USE

Four cards: a clean fit, a boundary case, a routing use, and a mistake

CLEAN FIT

Converting retrieval scores to relevance weights

b03's scores become [0.659, 0.242, 0.099] at T=1 — a real distribution usable directly as soft relevance weights across the three candidates.

BOUNDARY CASE

All scores identical

If z = [1.0, 1.0, 1.0], every exponential is equal, so softmax returns exactly [1/3, 1/3, 1/3] at any temperature — the uniform distribution, correctly, with no candidate favored.

ROUTING USE

Choosing among tools or indexes

A query router scoring three candidate tools can use a low temperature to commit strongly to the top-scoring tool, or a higher one to keep a fallback option's probability meaningfully above zero.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Applying softmax across the wrong axis

Softmax must be applied across the set of competing candidates for one query — applying it down a single candidate's own feature vector instead produces a distribution over the wrong thing entirely.

12
CODE

Softmax and the log-sum-exp trick, from scratch

PYTHON · NO LIBRARIES 
import math

def softmax(scores, T=1.0):
    # Section 09's log-sum-exp trick -- subtract the max before exponentiating
    m = max(scores)
    scaled = [(s - m) / T for s in scores]
    exps = [math.exp(s) for s in scaled]
    total = sum(exps)
    return [e / total for e in exps]

scores = [2.0, 1.0, 0.1]

p_t1   = softmax(scores, T=1.0)     # [0.659, 0.242, 0.099]
p_t05  = softmax(scores, T=0.5)     # [0.864, 0.117, 0.019]
p_t2   = softmax(scores, T=2.0)     # [0.502, 0.304, 0.194]

print([round(p, 3) for p in p_t1])
assert abs(sum(p_t1) - 1.0) < 1e-9                       # Section 05's requirement
assert p_t1.index(max(p_t1)) == scores.index(max(scores))  # Section 09's ranking-preservation claim

# overflow check: without the max-subtraction trick this would raise OverflowError
huge_scores = [1000.0, 999.0, 1.0]
p_huge = softmax(huge_scores, T=1.0)
print([round(p, 6) for p in p_huge])                     # [0.731, 0.269, ~0.0] -- computed safely

ECOSYSTEM

Where production code exposes the same mechanism

PyTorch's torch.nn.functional.softmax(logits, dim=-1) and its temperature-scaled variants apply exactly Section 05's formula, over whichever axis dim names — the same axis-choice caution as Section 11's mistake to avoid. NumPy and SciPy expose scipy.special.softmax, and both libraries' internal implementations use Section 09's log-sum-exp shift by default, never the naive unshifted version. Any language model's decoding step — temperature-controlled sampling included — applies this identical formula to the vocabulary's raw logits at every generated token; a decoding temperature of 0.7 versus 1.2 is the same dial as this concept's T, just applied to a much longer list of scores.

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes — of computing and using softmax

NUMERICAL OVERFLOW

Exponentiating a large raw score directly

exp(1000) overflows standard floating-point outright; Section 09's log-sum-exp shift is the fix, not an optional refinement.

TEMPERATURE SET TOO LOW

Softmax collapsing toward a near one-hot output

An extremely small T makes softmax behave almost exactly like argmax, discarding the gradient signal Concept 08's training loss actually needs from the runner-up candidates.

TEMPERATURE SET TOO HIGH

Softmax collapsing toward uniform

An extremely large T washes out real score differences entirely, making every candidate look equally likely even when the raw scores clearly favored one.

WRONG AXIS

Normalizing over the wrong dimension

Section 11's mistake to avoid, stated as a failure mode: applying softmax down a single candidate's own feature vector instead of across competing candidates silently produces a meaningless distribution.

CORRECTION

Misconceptions

Incorrect beliefCorrection
Higher temperature means a more confident outputThe opposite — higher T flattens the distribution toward uniform; lower T sharpens it toward the top score (Section 07, Section 10).
Softmax can re-rank its inputs at some temperatureIt cannot, for any T > 0 — Section 09 proves this directly from the monotonicity of exp and of positive division.

18
REALITY

Production implications, at introductory depth

  • Temperature is a genuine, frequently tuned hyperparameter — in generation (decoding temperature), in distillation (Section 24, forward-referenced literature), and in contrastive training objectives (Concept 08, forward reference), each with its own preferred range.
  • Every serious softmax implementation uses the log-sum-exp shift by default (Section 13) — a from-scratch implementation that skips it will work in a classroom example and silently overflow the first time scores get large in production.
  • Because ranking is provably preserved (Section 09), a retrieval system's final top-k ordering can be computed directly from raw similarity scores without ever running softmax at inference time — softmax only needs to be computed where an actual probability or a differentiable loss is required, such as during training (Concept 08).

RECALL

Knowledge check

State the softmax formula with temperature, and the range T can take.
softmax_T(zᵢ) = exp(zᵢ/T) / Σⱼ exp(zⱼ/T), defined for any T > 0.
Does raising temperature ever change which candidate has the highest softmax output? Why or why not?
No — Section 09 proves that for any T > 0, dividing by T, exponentiating, and normalizing are all order-preserving operations, so the ranking of the outputs always matches the ranking of the raw scores.
Why does a real softmax implementation subtract the maximum score before exponentiating?
To avoid floating-point overflow on large scores (Section 09's log-sum-exp trick) — the shift is algebraically exact and changes nothing about the final probabilities.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Explain why softmax is described as a "soft" version of argmax, and what is lost by using argmax directly during training.
Softmax keeps every candidate's probability strictly positive and differentiable with respect to the raw scores, so a gradient-based optimizer can push scores up or down smoothly. Argmax's output is a discontinuous, non-differentiable one-hot vector — a training loss built directly on it would have no useful gradient almost everywhere (Section 15).
Prove that softmax preserves ranking for any positive temperature.
For zᵢ > zⱼ and T > 0: zᵢ/T > zⱼ/T (dividing by a positive number preserves order); exp(zᵢ/T) > exp(zⱼ/T) (exp is strictly increasing); dividing both by the same positive sum S preserves the inequality — so softmax_T(zᵢ) > softmax_T(zⱼ) (Section 09).
Design question: a production softmax implementation is overflowing on a batch of unusually large raw scores. What is the fix, and why does it not change the output?
Subtract the maximum score in the batch from every score before exponentiating (the log-sum-exp trick, Section 09). This makes the largest exponent exactly 0 and every other exponent negative, so no term can overflow; algebraically, the shift cancels exactly in the final ratio, so the computed probabilities are identical to the unshifted (but overflow-prone) version.
