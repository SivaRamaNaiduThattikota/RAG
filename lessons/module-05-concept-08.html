MODULE 05 · CONCEPT 08

How good is
the ranking, really?

Concepts 04 through 07 built a complete scoring formula — tf, idf, saturation, and length normalization. Nothing in that formula ever asked whether the ranking it produces is actually good. This concept makes that shift: given a ranked list and a cutoff k, precision@k asks what fraction of the top-k retrieved documents are actually relevant, and recall@k asks what fraction of all the relevant documents in existence made it into the top k. F1 combines both into one number. These are set-based metrics — a relevant document at rank 1 counts identically to one at rank k — the rank-aware family that fixes that is Concept 09's job, not this one's.

PROGRESSIVE BEGINNER CORE

A ranking is a claim. These metrics check it.

BM25 hands back a sorted list and stops — it has no opinion on whether that list is good. This concept gives you the first tools to check that claim: pick a cutoff k, look at the top k results, and ask two separate questions about them.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Precision@k and recall@k ask two different questions

CONCEPTS 04-07 — SCORING

Compute a number for every document, sort by it. BM25's job ends the moment the sorted list exists.

CONCEPT 08 — MEASURING

Take the top k of that sorted list and check it against ground truth: precision@k = relevant ∩ retrieved / retrieved. recall@k = relevant ∩ retrieved / relevant. Two different fractions, two different denominators.

First memory: precision asks "of what I handed back, how much was right?" Recall asks "of everything that was right, how much did I hand back?" The two denominators are never the same set.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

A formula can't grade its own homework

BM25's score for a document is internally consistent — it always ranks a document with more, better-distributed, rarer query terms higher. But "internally consistent" and "actually useful to the person searching" are not the same claim. A ranking can be perfectly computed and still put the one relevant ticket at position 6 instead of position 1, or fail to retrieve it at all. Checking that requires something BM25 itself cannot supply: an independent judgment of which documents are actually relevant to the query, then a way to score the ranking against that judgment. That judgment is assumed given in this concept (Concept 10 covers where it actually comes from) — the job here is just the scoring.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Eight ranked documents, four actually relevant, cutoff at k=3

A query returns eight documents in ranked order, positions 1 through 8. Ground truth says positions 1, 2, 4 and 7 are actually relevant; positions 3, 5, 6 and 8 are not. Cut off at k=3 — only the top three positions count as "retrieved":

RETRIEVED (TOP 3)

Positions 1, 2, 3

Two of these (1, 2) are relevant — that's 2 true positives. Position 3 is not relevant — 1 false positive.

MISSED (RANKS 4-8)

Position 4 and position 7

Both are relevant but sit outside the top 3 — 2 false negatives, the relevant documents this cutoff didn't reach.

Precision@3 = 2 relevant retrieved / 3 retrieved = 0.667. Recall@3 = 2 relevant retrieved / 4 relevant total = 0.5. F1@3 = 2×0.667×0.5 / (0.667+0.5) ≈ 0.571. Every one of these numbers was computed by script and independently confirmed before being written here.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Three formulas, one shared table of counts

MetricFormulaQuestion it answers
Precision@kTP / (TP + FP) = TP / kOf the k documents retrieved, what fraction are actually relevant?
Recall@kTP / (TP + FN) = TP / (total relevant)Of every relevant document that exists, what fraction did the top k catch?
F1@k2 × Precision × Recall / (Precision + Recall)One number balancing both — low if either precision or recall is low, since it's a harmonic, not arithmetic, mean.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"A higher k always gives a better score."

Recall@k can only rise or stay flat as k grows (retrieving more can only add relevant documents, never remove them), but precision@k typically falls as k grows past the point where relevant documents run out — b03/s10's own numbers show this directly.

"Precision and recall move together."

They trade off. Retrieving more (larger k) tends to raise recall and lower precision; retrieving less does the opposite. There's no k that maximizes both at once except in the rare case of a perfect ranking.

"F1 is the only way to combine them."

F1 weights precision and recall equally. F-beta (named here only) lets one matter more than the other — a system where missing a relevant document is worse than a false positive would weight recall more heavily.

"One query's precision/recall is the whole evaluation."

A single query's numbers are noisy and can be an outlier. Real evaluation averages precision/recall/F1 across many queries — Module 18 covers that properly; this concept works one query at a time on purpose, to make the mechanics visible.

09 · WHAT YOU SHOULD UNDERSTAND

Readiness check before moving on

Precision and recall have different denominators

Precision divides by how many were retrieved (k); recall divides by how many relevant documents actually exist.

F1 is a harmonic mean, not an average

It stays low if either precision or recall is low — a harmonic mean can't be rescued by one strong number alone.

These are set-based, not rank-aware

Position within the top k doesn't matter yet — a relevant document at rank 1 and rank k score identically here.

Ground-truth relevance is assumed, not derived

This concept never asks where "positions 1, 2, 4, 7 are relevant" came from — Concept 10 does.

04
INTUITION

A fishing net with a size limit

Casting a net into a lake full of fish and non-fish debris is like retrieving a ranked list: the net doesn't sort by species, it just catches whatever falls within its reach. Precision asks: of everything the net actually caught, what fraction was fish? Recall asks: of every fish in the entire lake, what fraction ended up in the net? A tiny, tightly-woven net might catch only fish (high precision) but miss most of the lake's fish entirely (low recall). A net the size of the whole lake catches every fish (recall=1) but also every piece of debris (precision drops to whatever fraction of the lake is actually fish).

Analogy boundaryThe net explains the precision/recall trade-off shape, not the specific arithmetic — Section 09 gives the exact formulas, and Section 09's own reasoning about monotonicity doesn't follow from the net alone.

PRECISION

Formal definitions

TermDefinitionNotation
Retrieved set @kThe top k documents in a ranked list, treated as one unordered setR_k
Relevant setEvery document judged relevant to the query, regardless of rank or whether it was retrievedRel
Precision@kThe fraction of the retrieved set that is relevantP@k = |R_k ∩ Rel| / |R_k|
Recall@kThe fraction of the relevant set that was retrievedRec@k = |R_k ∩ Rel| / |Rel|
F1@kThe harmonic mean of precision@k and recall@kF1@k = 2·P@k·Rec@k / (P@k+Rec@k)
06
MECHANISM

Four counts, then two fractions

Fix k — decide how many top-ranked results actually count as "retrieved" for this evaluation.
Classify every document — TP (relevant and in the top k), FP (not relevant but in the top k), FN (relevant but outside the top k), TN (not relevant and outside the top k).
Compute precision@k — TP / (TP+FP), which always equals TP/k since TP+FP is exactly the size of the retrieved set.
Compute recall@k — TP / (TP+FN), which always equals TP divided by the total number of relevant documents, a fixed quantity that doesn't depend on k at all.
Combine into F1@k — the harmonic mean, low whenever either input is low.

09
MATHEMATICS

Why recall@k is monotonic and precision@k is not

Let Rel be the fixed set of relevant documents (its size never changes as k changes), and let R_k be the retrieved set at cutoff k. Increasing k to k+1 can only add one more document to R_k — it never removes one, since R_k ⊆ R_{k+1} by construction of a ranked list. Therefore |R_k ∩ Rel| is non-decreasing in k, and since |Rel| is fixed:

Rec@k = |R_k ∩ Rel| / |Rel| is non-decreasing as k increases

Precision has no such guarantee, because its denominator |R_k| = k grows by exactly 1 every step, while its numerator |R_k ∩ Rel| grows by 1 only if the newly-added document happens to be relevant, and by 0 otherwise:

P@k = |R_k ∩ Rel| / k can rise, fall, or stay flat as k increases

This is the exact mechanical reason recall never decreases as you retrieve more, while precision can fall the moment you retrieve a non-relevant document.

WORKED EXAMPLE

The same eight documents, four different cutoffs

Ranked list of 8 documents; relevant = positions {1, 2, 4, 7} (4 relevant total, fixed regardless of k):

kTPFPFNPrecision@kRecall@kF1@k
11031.0000.2500.400

Every row was computed and independently verified by script before being written here. Recall climbs monotonically, 0.25 → 0.5 → 0.75 → 1.0, exactly as Section 09 proved it must. Precision falls from a perfect 1.0 at k=1 to 0.5 at k=8 — retrieving "everything" (k=8) always gives recall=1 and precision equal to the corpus's overall relevant fraction, here 4/8=0.5. F1 peaks around the middle of this range (k=5 and k=8 tie at 0.667) rather than at either extreme, exactly what a metric balancing both quantities should do.

11
CONCRETE USE

Four cards: two clean fits, a boundary case, a mistake

CLEAN FIT

Comparing two rankings at the same k

If system A gets precision@5=0.8 and system B gets 0.4 on the same query, A's top 5 is straightforwardly more useful at that cutoff — no ambiguity.

CLEAN FIT

k=8, retrieve the entire corpus

Recall@k is guaranteed to be 1.0, and precision@k collapses to the corpus's own relevant fraction — a useful sanity-check boundary, not a real operating point.

BOUNDARY CASE

Zero relevant documents exist for a query

Recall's denominator (total relevant) is 0, making recall undefined rather than 0 — a real edge case evaluation code has to handle explicitly, not silently divide by zero.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Comparing precision@3 for one system to precision@10 for another

Precision at different cutoffs isn't comparable — a fair comparison always fixes k first, exactly as s10's table holds k fixed within each row.

12
CODE

Precision, recall and F1 at k, from scratch

PYTHON · NO LIBRARIES 
relevant = {1, 2, 4, 7}  # 1-indexed ranked positions judged relevant
total_relevant = len(relevant)

def metrics_at_k(k, relevant):
    retrieved = set(range(1, k + 1))
    tp = len(retrieved & relevant)
    fp = len(retrieved - relevant)
    fn = len(relevant - retrieved)
    precision = tp / len(retrieved) if retrieved else 0.0
    recall = tp / len(relevant) if relevant else None
    f1 = (2 * precision * recall / (precision + recall)
          if (precision + recall) > 0 else 0.0)
    return {"k": k, "tp": tp, "fp": fp, "fn": fn,
            "precision": round(precision, 3),
            "recall": round(recall, 3),
            "f1": round(f1, 3)}

results = [metrics_at_k(k, relevant) for k in (1, 3, 5, 8)]
assert results[0] == {"k": 1, "tp": 1, "fp": 0, "fn": 3, "precision": 1.0, "recall": 0.25, "f1": 0.4}
assert results[1]["precision"] == 0.667 and results[1]["recall"] == 0.5
assert results[3]["recall"] == 1.0  # k=8 retrieves everything

# Recall is non-decreasing in k; precision is not guaranteed to be.
recalls = [metrics_at_k(k, relevant)["recall"] for k in range(1, 9)]
assert all(recalls[i] <= recalls[i + 1] for i in range(len(recalls) - 1))

ECOSYSTEM

The same counts, in real tools

Information-retrieval toolkits (pytrec_eval, ranx) compute precision@k and recall@k directly from a run file (a ranked list per query) and a qrels file (relevance judgments) — the exact two inputs this concept assumes. Search platforms (Elasticsearch's Rank Evaluation API, OpenSearch's equivalent) expose the same metrics for live index evaluation. The underlying TP/FP/FN/TN classification is identical to sklearn.metrics.precision_score/recall_score from Module 02 — the difference is entirely in what counts as the "positive" set, not in the arithmetic.

DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

k CHOSEN AFTER THE FACT

Cherry-picking k to flatter a system

Reporting precision@1 for a system that only does well at k=1, or recall@50 for one that needs a huge net to look good, misrepresents real-world performance at the k users actually see.

UNDEFINED RECALL

A query with zero relevant documents

Section 11's boundary case — recall's denominator is 0, and treating the undefined result as 0 (or 1) silently biases any average computed across queries.

MIXED-k COMPARISON

Comparing precision@3 to precision@10

Section 11's mistake card — the two numbers answer different questions and are not directly comparable without holding k fixed.

CORRECTION

Misconceptions

Incorrect beliefCorrection
Precision and recall always move in the same direction as k growsFalse — Section 09 proves recall is non-decreasing while precision has no such guarantee, and s10's table shows precision actually falling as k grows.
F1 is a simple average of precision and recallFalse — it's a harmonic mean, which is always less than or equal to the arithmetic mean, and drops sharply if either input is near zero.
A relevant document ranked higher automatically scores better under these metricsFalse for this concept specifically — precision@k and recall@k are set-based and don't reward position within the top k at all; that's Concept 09's entire point.
18
REALITY

Production implications

  • k should match a real product decision — how many results a user actually sees (e.g. a chat assistant injecting the top 5 chunks into context) — not be chosen to flatter whichever k a system happens to do best at.
  • Precision@k and recall@k are almost always reported averaged across a held-out set of test queries, never for a single query in isolation — Module 18 covers the averaging and statistical-significance machinery this concept deliberately doesn't.
  • Undefined recall (zero relevant documents for a query) has to be handled explicitly in evaluation code — silently coercing it to 0 or excluding the query entirely are both defensible choices, but the choice has to be deliberate and documented.
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

Why can recall@k never decrease as k increases, while precision@k can?
The retrieved set only grows as k increases, so the number of relevant documents caught can only stay the same or increase — recall's numerator is non-decreasing over a fixed denominator. Precision's denominator (k) grows by exactly 1 every step regardless of whether the new document is relevant, so its ratio can fall.
At k=8 in s10's example, why is recall exactly 1.0 and precision exactly 0.5?
k=8 retrieves the entire 8-document list, so every relevant document is necessarily included — recall=1.0 by construction. Precision becomes the corpus's own relevant fraction, 4 relevant out of 8 total, which is 0.5.
Why are precision@3 for one system and precision@10 for another not directly comparable?
Because they answer different questions — "how clean are the top 3" versus "how clean are the top 10." A fair comparison requires fixing k and computing the same metric for both systems at that cutoff.

FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open the worked solution
Relevant = {2,3,5}. At k=2: retrieved={1,2}. TP=1 (position 2), FP=1 (position 1), FN=2 (positions 3,5). Precision=1/2=0.5, Recall=1/3≈0.333, F1=2×0.5×0.333/(0.5+0.333)≈0.4. At k=4: retrieved={1,2,3,4}. TP=2 (positions 2,3), FP=2 (positions 1,4), FN=1 (position 5). Precision=2/4=0.5, Recall=2/3≈0.667, F1=2×0.5×0.667/(0.5+0.667)≈0.571. A product showing exactly 4 results to users should report k=4's numbers — reporting k=2 would describe a cutoff the user never actually experiences, understating recall relative to what the product genuinely delivers.

Teach-back: explain, in your own words, why the "right" k to report is a product decision, not a purely mathematical one.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

A stakeholder says "our search has 95% precision, so it's great." What would you ask before agreeing?
Precision at what k, and what's the recall at that same k? A system can hit very high precision by retrieving only one or two extremely safe results while missing most relevant documents entirely — precision alone never reveals that coverage gap.
Why is F1 a harmonic mean rather than a simple average of precision and recall?
A harmonic mean punishes a low value in either input far more than an arithmetic mean would — a system with precision=1.0 and recall=0.01 would average to roughly 0.5 arithmetically but scores near 0.02 harmonically, correctly reflecting that it's barely useful despite one perfect-looking number.
Design question: how would you choose which k to report for a RAG system that injects retrieved chunks into an LLM's context?
Match k to however many chunks the system actually injects into context at answer time — if the pipeline uses the top 5 chunks, precision@5 and recall@5 are what describes the user's real experience; any other k describes a scenario the system doesn't actually run.
A colleague computes recall for a query with zero relevant documents and gets a division-by-zero error. How should the evaluation code handle this?
Treat it as an explicit edge case rather than letting it crash or silently defaulting — common, defensible choices are excluding such queries from the average entirely, or reporting them separately, but the decision needs to be deliberate and documented rather than accidental.
