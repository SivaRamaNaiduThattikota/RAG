01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Prompts, decoding, and generated output

A prompt is the tokenized input handed to the model—often structured into roles such as system, user, and assistant rather than one plain block of text. Given that input, the model produces a probability distribution over every token in its vocabulary for what should come next.

Decoding is the rule that turns that distribution into one chosen token. Generation repeats prediction, decoding, and appending—one token at a time—until a stop condition ends the sequence, which is then detokenized back into readable text.

First memory: one model call predicts a distribution; decoding and looping produce the sentence.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Why not generate the whole answer in one shot?

The model was trained to predict one next token given everything before it—that is its only output shape. A stop condition is required, or generation would continue until an arbitrary length limit. A decoding rule is required because a probability distribution is not yet a choice: something must turn “12: 34%, 10: 22%, several: 15%…” into an actual next token.

DETERMINISTIC NEED

Factual, citation-bearing answers usually want the same input to produce a consistent answer.

DIVERSITY NEED

Brainstorming or varied phrasing benefits from controlled randomness across calls.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Tracing “The leave policy allows…”

After the prompt “The leave policy allows”, the model’s distribution favors 12 over 10, several, many, and unlimited. Greedy decoding always takes the single highest-probability token at each step; sampling can occasionally take a lower-probability one instead.

STEP 1

Distribution over the next token; greedy picks 12.

STEP 2

12 is appended; the new distribution favors days.

STEP 3

days is appended; the distribution now favors the stop token .—generation ends.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

How a distribution becomes a token

GREEDY

Always highest probability

Deterministic; identical input always produces the identical output.

TEMPERATURE SAMPLING

Reweight, then sample

Scales the distribution before sampling; higher temperature flattens it toward more randomness.

TOP-K

Sample among the k best

Restricts sampling to the k highest-probability tokens, then renormalizes.

TOP-P (NUCLEUS)

Sample within a probability mass

Keeps the smallest set of tokens whose probabilities sum to at least p.

BEAM SEARCH

Track multiple sequences

Keeps several candidate continuations in parallel to favor overall sequence likelihood.

STOP CONDITIONS

End the loop

An end-of-sequence token, a stop string, or a maximum token count.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Terms that get blurred together

Term What it actually is Common confusion
Prompt The tokenized input for one model call Confused with the entire conversation history
Distribution Probabilities the model assigns to every vocabulary token Confused with the chosen token itself
Decoding The rule that turns a distribution into a token Confused with the model’s knowledge or accuracy
Temperature A reweighting applied before sampling Confused with randomness in general, including top-k/top-p

Two calls with identical input and greedy decoding always produce the same output; two calls with identical input and sampling can legitimately differ.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often misunderstand

“The model writes the whole answer in one step.”

It predicts one token at a time inside a repeating loop.

“Temperature changes what the model knows.”

It only changes how a token is picked from the same underlying distribution.

“Higher temperature always improves answers.”

Past a point it increases incoherence, not quality.

“Greedy decoding is always worse than sampling.”

Greedy is often preferred for consistent, citation-bearing answers.

“A system prompt guarantees the model will obey it.”

It strongly conditions the distribution but does not guarantee compliance.

“Stopping is automatic and always correct.”

Missing or misconfigured stop conditions can truncate or run past a useful answer.

08 · YOUR PROJECT MAPPING

Choosing decoding settings for Oracle RAG

FACTUAL / CITED ANSWERS Low temperature or greedy

Favors consistent wording tied to the retrieved evidence.

EXPLORATORY ASSISTANCE Moderate temperature or top-p

Allows varied phrasing when strict consistency is not required.

STRUCTURED OUTPUT Stop sequences and schema constraints

Prevent generation from continuing past a required JSON or citation block.

PROMPT ROLES Separate instructions, evidence, and question

System role carries policy; retrieved context stays clearly bounded from the user’s question.

LangChain and LangGraph expose decoding parameters and stop sequences as configuration, but the underlying distribution still comes from the model, and the decoding rule still decides what leaves that distribution as a token.

04
INTUITION

One word at a time, never looking back to rewrite

Think of the model as writing one word at a time on a typewriter with no backspace key: at every position it weighs every possible next character against everything already typed, commits to one, and moves forward. Decoding is the rule for which weighted option gets committed.

Analogy boundaryA typewriter commits letter by letter with no lookahead; a decoding rule can still consider many candidate tokens at each step before committing to exactly one—it just cannot revise a token once it has been appended.

08
ARCHITECTURE

Generation is the last stage of the RAG pipeline

Retrieved chunks ↓ Context + question assembled ↓ Prompt tokenized ↓ Decoding loop ↓ Generated tokens ↓ Detokenized answer

Everything upstream—retrieval quality, context construction, prompt roles—only shapes the distribution the model produces. Decoding then decides what leaves that distribution as the visible answer.

09
MATHEMATICS

Softmax with temperature, and top-k/top-p

Softmax with temperature: P(token_i) = exp(z_i / T) / Σ_j exp(z_j / T)

z_i is the logit for token i and T is the temperature. As T → 0, the distribution concentrates on the single highest logit, converging toward greedy decoding; as T grows, the distribution flattens toward uniform.

Top-k: keep the k tokens with highest P, renormalize, then sample
Top-p: keep the smallest token set S where Σ_{i∈S} P(token_i) ≥ p, renormalize, then sample
10
WORKED EXAMPLE

Temperature by hand on three logits

Logits z = [2.0, 1.0, 0.0] for tokens [A, B, C].

T=1: exp(2)=7.39, exp(1)=2.72, exp(0)=1.00; sum=11.11 → P=[0.665, 0.245, 0.090]
T=2: exp(1)=2.72, exp(.5)=1.65, exp(0)=1.00; sum=5.37 → P=[0.507, 0.307, 0.186]

Raising the temperature from 1 to 2 pulled probability away from the top token (0.665 → 0.507) and toward the others—the ranking is unchanged, but the distribution is flatter, so sampling is more likely to pick B or C.

12
CODE

Greedy versus temperature sampling from scratch

PYTHON · FROM-SCRATCH PSEUDOCODE 
import math, random

def softmax_with_temperature(logits, temperature):
    scaled = [logit / temperature for logit in logits]
    m = max(scaled)
    exps = [math.exp(value - m) for value in scaled]
    total = sum(exps)
    return [value / total for value in exps]

def decode_step(logits, temperature=None):
    if temperature is None:
        return logits.index(max(logits))          # greedy
    probs = softmax_with_temperature(logits, temperature)
    u, cumulative = random.random(), 0.0
    for index, p in enumerate(probs):
        cumulative += p
        if u < cumulative:
            return index
    return len(probs) - 1

Provider decoding parameters

Model APIs and libraries such as Hugging Face transformers.generate expose temperature, top-k, top-p, repetition penalties, and stop sequences as call-time arguments rather than model retraining. LangChain and LangGraph pass these same parameters through to the underlying provider call—they configure decoding, they do not implement a different decoding algorithm of their own.

CONTRAST

Choosing a decoding strategy

Strategy Determinism Best fit
Greedy Fully deterministic Consistent, citation-bearing answers
Temperature sampling Stochastic, tunable spread Controlled variety across calls
Top-k / top-p Stochastic, bounded candidate set Variety while avoiding very low-probability tokens
Beam search Deterministic given beam width Favoring overall sequence likelihood over per-token greed
16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

REPETITION

Degenerate loops

Greedy or low-temperature decoding can repeat phrases without a repetition penalty.

INCOHERENCE

Temperature too high

Excessive randomness produces fluent-looking but unreliable text.

RUNAWAY LENGTH

Missing stop condition

Generation continues to the maximum length instead of a natural end.

TRUNCATION

Stop sequence fires too early

A stop string that also appears inside valid content cuts the answer short.

INCONSISTENCY

Unintended sampling

Sampling left on for tasks that actually need reproducible, citation-bearing answers.

PROMPT LEAKAGE

Role boundaries ignored

Generated text echoes or is steered by content that should have stayed in the system role.

17
CORRECTION

Misconceptions

Incorrect belief Correction
Temperature=0 and greedy are two unrelated settings Temperature approaching 0 converges toward greedy behavior mathematically.
Sampling makes the model know more Sampling only changes which already-assigned probability becomes the chosen token.
Beam search always beats greedy It optimizes overall sequence likelihood, which does not guarantee better factual or grounded answers.
Stop tokens are optional detail A missing or wrong stop condition directly causes truncated or runaway generation.
18
REALITY

Production implications

  • Fix decoding parameters and log them per request for reproducibility during debugging.
  • Use low temperature or greedy decoding for answers that must match approved wording or citations.
  • Set explicit stop sequences for structured outputs; verify they cannot appear inside valid content.
  • Cap maximum generated tokens to bound latency and cost even when a stop condition should fire earlier.
  • Monitor for repetition and truncation as separate, distinct failure signals.
  • Treat decoding settings as part of the versioned configuration, not an implementation detail.
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

What does temperature mathematically do to logits?
It divides each logit before softmax, flattening the resulting distribution as temperature increases and sharpening it as temperature decreases toward zero.
Why can identical prompts produce different answers?
A stochastic decoding rule such as temperature sampling or top-k/top-p can select a different token at any step, even from an identical distribution.
What ends the generation loop?
An end-of-sequence token, a configured stop string, or a maximum token length.
Why might beam search not be the best fit for a RAG answer?
It optimizes for overall sequence likelihood, not for grounding in retrieved evidence, so it can still produce fluent but ungrounded text.

FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open one defensible decision pattern
Policy answers: greedy or very low temperature, stop at the citation block, to keep wording consistent and auditable. Taglines: moderate-to-high temperature or top-p, no strict stop sequence beyond a length cap, to encourage variety. Data extraction: low temperature, an explicit stop sequence or schema-validated stop, since malformed structure is worse than reduced variety. Brainstorming: higher temperature or top-k, generous length, since a wider range of coherent but lower-probability ideas is the goal. The opposite choice in each case trades away the property the task actually needed—consistency for the first two, variety for the last two.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Explain the difference between top-k and top-p sampling.
Top-k restricts sampling to a fixed number of highest-probability tokens; top-p restricts sampling to the smallest set of tokens whose cumulative probability reaches a threshold, so its effective candidate count varies with how peaked the distribution is.
Why would you fix decoding parameters for a production RAG endpoint?
To keep answers reproducible and debuggable, and to avoid unintended variation in citation-bearing or policy-sensitive responses.
What happens mathematically to softmax output as temperature approaches zero?
The distribution concentrates almost entirely on the token with the highest logit, converging to greedy decoding’s behavior.
How would you debug a model that generates endlessly without stopping?
Check whether the expected end-of-sequence or stop-string condition is configured and reachable, and confirm the maximum-length cap is acting only as a backstop, not the primary stop mechanism.
