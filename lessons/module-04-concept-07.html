MODULE 04 · CONCEPT 07

Entropy
and cross-entropy.

Concept 06 turned any list of raw scores into a genuine probability distribution, and proved that temperature reshapes it without ever changing which candidate ranks highest. This concept asks two separate questions about a distribution like that. Entropy looks at the distribution alone: how spread out or how peaked is it? Cross-entropy looks at the distribution against a stated truth: given where the real answer actually landed, how well did this distribution see it coming? Cross-entropy is the exact number Concept 08's training objective minimizes.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Entropy measures a distribution alone; cross-entropy measures it against a truth

ENTROPY, H(P)

H(P) = −Σᵢ pᵢ·ln(pᵢ). A property of one distribution, by itself — no "correct answer" needed to compute it. Low entropy means concentrated on a few outcomes; high entropy means spread thin.

CROSS-ENTROPY, H(P,Q)

H(P,Q) = −Σᵢ pᵢ·ln(qᵢ), comparing a true distribution P against a predicted one Q. When exactly one outcome is actually correct — P is one-hot — this collapses to a single term: −ln(q_correct).

First memory: entropy asks "how spread out is this distribution?"; cross-entropy asks "how surprised should I be, given this distribution, when the real answer turns out to be this one?"
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Without this, "confident" and "correct" get confused

Concept 06's distribution can be as sharp or as flat as temperature allows, but sharpness alone says nothing about whether the sharp end is actually pointed at the right candidate. Entropy measures the sharpness. Cross-entropy is the number that catches the case a raw glance at the bars can't: a distribution that looks confident and is confidently wrong.

ENTROPY ALONE

A distribution at T=0.5 has low entropy — it's sharp, concentrated on one candidate. That's a fact about the distribution's shape, true regardless of which candidate is actually correct.

ENTROPY PLUS CROSS-ENTROPY

The same sharp distribution scores a tiny cross-entropy if the sharp end happens to be the correct candidate — and a large one if it isn't. Sharpness cuts both ways.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Concept 06's own distribution, measured for spread

Concept 06's three candidates at T=1 gave p = [0.659, 0.242, 0.099] — cited directly, not recomputed. Entropy sums −pᵢ·ln(pᵢ) over all three terms.

TERM FOR DOC 1

−0.659 × ln(0.659) = −0.659 × (−0.4166) ≈ 0.2746.

TERM FOR DOC 2

−0.242 × ln(0.242) = −0.242 × (−1.4188) ≈ 0.3434.

TERM FOR DOC 3

−0.099 × ln(0.099) = −0.099 × (−2.3125) ≈ 0.2289.

SUM = ENTROPY

0.2746 + 0.3434 + 0.2289 ≈ 0.8467 nats.

Every term is positive (each pᵢ ∈ (0,1), so ln(pᵢ) is negative, and the leading minus sign flips it back positive) — entropy itself is always non-negative, for any distribution.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

The same distribution, scored against two different truths

IF DOC 1 IS ACTUALLY CORRECT

Cross-entropy = −ln(0.659) ≈ 0.4170 nats

The distribution's largest share landed on the right candidate — a small, low loss.

IF DOC 3 IS ACTUALLY CORRECT

Cross-entropy = −ln(0.099) ≈ 2.3170 nats

Same exact distribution — but the truth landed on the candidate it favored least. The loss jumps more than five-fold.

WHY IT COLLAPSES TO ONE TERM

The true distribution is one-hot

P has a 1 at the correct index and 0 everywhere else; every 0×ln(qᵢ) term vanishes, leaving only −1×ln(q_correct) (Section 09 proves the general identity behind this).

WHAT STAYS FIXED

Entropy doesn't change

H(P) ≈ 0.8467 nats either way — entropy only depends on the distribution's own shape, never on which candidate later turns out to be correct.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Entropy, cross-entropy, and KL divergence — three related, different numbers

TermWhat it actually measuresCommon confusion
Entropy, H(P)How spread out ONE distribution is, with no reference to any truthMistaken for a correctness score — it isn't; a confidently wrong distribution can still have very low entropy
Cross-entropy, H(P,Q)How well a predicted distribution Q agrees with a true distribution PAssumed symmetric — H(P,Q) ≠ H(Q,P) in general; here P is fixed as the one-hot truth, Q as the softmax prediction
KL divergence, KL(P‖Q)The extra cost specifically caused by using Q instead of the true P — KL(P‖Q) = H(P,Q) − H(P)Treated as identical to cross-entropy — they coincide only when H(P) = 0, exactly the one-hot-truth case this module always uses (Section 09)
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"Low entropy means the prediction is probably correct."

Entropy never looks at the truth at all — b04's doc-3 case has the exact same low-ish entropy as the doc-1 case, with a cross-entropy over five times larger.

"Entropy and cross-entropy are just two names for the same formula."

Entropy takes one distribution as input; cross-entropy takes two — a true one and a predicted one (Section 05).

"You need a ground-truth label before you can compute entropy."

Entropy is defined on a single distribution alone — b03 computes it with no mention of which candidate is correct.

"Cross-entropy can be negative when a prediction is very good."

It can get arbitrarily close to 0 as q_correct → 1, but −ln(x) is never negative for x ∈ (0,1] — cross-entropy is always ≥ 0.

04
INTUITION

Surprise, and the price of being confidently wrong

Think of −ln(p) as a "surprise" score for a single outcome: an outcome you assigned probability close to 1 barely surprises you at all when it happens (−ln(p) near 0); an outcome you assigned a tiny probability surprises you a great deal (−ln(p) large). Entropy is the surprise you'd expect on average, if the world really does behave according to your own distribution. Cross-entropy is the surprise you actually get, when the world behaves according to a different, true distribution instead — and it is never smaller than entropy, because being wrong about where reality lands only ever costs you extra surprise, never a discount.

Analogy boundary"Surprise" is an intuition for −ln(p), not a rigorous definition — Section 09's identity is the exact statement of why cross-entropy can never fall below entropy.

MECHANISM

Tracing entropy and cross-entropy for the same distribution

Start with Concept 06's distribution — p = [0.659, 0.242, 0.099] at T=1.
Entropy — take −pᵢ·ln(pᵢ) for each term — [0.2746, 0.3434, 0.2289], summing to ≈0.8467 nats.
Cross-entropy, truth = doc 1 — P = [1,0,0], so only the first term survives: −1·ln(0.659) ≈ 0.4170 nats.
Cross-entropy, truth = doc 3 — P = [0,0,1], so only the third term survives: −1·ln(0.099) ≈ 2.3170 nats.
Compare — entropy (0.8467) never moves; cross-entropy swings from 0.4170 to 2.3170 depending only on where the truth actually is.

09
MATHEMATICS

H(P,Q) = H(P) + KL(P‖Q), and why it collapses for a one-hot truth

Cross-entropy decomposes exactly into entropy plus KL divergence:

H(P,Q) = −Σᵢ pᵢ·ln(qᵢ) = −Σᵢ pᵢ·ln(pᵢ) + Σᵢ pᵢ·ln(pᵢ/qᵢ) = H(P) + KL(P‖Q)

Whenever the true distribution P is one-hot — exactly one candidate is actually correct, as in every case this module uses — H(P) = 0 (there is a single term 1·ln(1) = 0, and every 0·ln(0) term vanishes by convention). The identity above then collapses to H(P,Q) = KL(P‖Q) = −ln(q_correct): cross-entropy against a one-hot truth is, exactly, the KL divergence from the truth to the prediction, with no separate entropy term left over.

11
CONCRETE USE

Four cards: a clean fit, a boundary case, a production use, and a mistake

CLEAN FIT

Cross-entropy as a retrieval loss

−ln(q_correct), computed per training query, is exactly the number Concept 08's objective drives toward 0 as the embedding model improves.

BOUNDARY CASE

The uniform distribution's maximum entropy

For n equally likely outcomes, entropy reaches its maximum, ln(n) — for three outcomes, ln(3) ≈ 1.0986 nats, matching Concept 06's uniform limit as T→∞.

PRODUCTION USE

Perplexity as exp(cross-entropy)

A commonly reported language-model metric, perplexity, is just exp() applied to an averaged cross-entropy — a familiar production number built directly on this concept's formula (forward reference, not derived here).

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Reporting entropy where cross-entropy was meant

A dashboard that only tracks a retrieval distribution's entropy is blind to b04's doc-3 failure mode entirely — entropy alone cannot detect a confidently wrong system.

12
CODE

Entropy and cross-entropy, from scratch

PYTHON · NO LIBRARIES 
import math

def entropy(probs):
    return -sum(p * math.log(p) for p in probs if p > 0)  # 0*ln(0) convention

def cross_entropy(true_idx, probs):
    return -math.log(probs[true_idx])  # one-hot truth collapses the sum to one term

probs = [0.659, 0.242, 0.099]  # Concept 06's distribution at T=1

h = entropy(probs)
print(round(h, 4))                          # 0.8467
assert abs(h - 0.8467) < 1e-3

ce_doc1 = cross_entropy(0, probs)
ce_doc3 = cross_entropy(2, probs)
print(round(ce_doc1, 4), round(ce_doc3, 4))  # 0.4170 2.3170
assert ce_doc3 > ce_doc1                     # confidently wrong costs more than confidently right
assert abs((ce_doc1) - (-math.log(probs[0]))) < 1e-9  # collapses to a single -ln(q_correct) term

# KL divergence, one-hot truth case: KL(P||Q) = H(P,Q) - H(P) = H(P,Q), since H(one-hot) = 0
kl_doc1 = ce_doc1 - 0.0
assert abs(kl_doc1 - ce_doc1) < 1e-9
13
ECOSYSTEM

Where production code exposes the same mechanism

PyTorch's torch.nn.CrossEntropyLoss takes raw logits directly, not a pre-computed softmax output — internally it applies a numerically stable log-softmax (Concept 06's log-sum-exp trick, applied to the log rather than computed as a separate softmax-then-log step) and then this concept's −ln(q_correct) in one fused operation, precisely to avoid computing softmax and its logarithm as two separate, less stable steps. scipy.stats.entropy computes Section 05's H(P) directly, and accepts a second distribution argument to compute cross-entropy or KL divergence between two distributions in one call.

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

CONFUSING ENTROPY WITH CORRECTNESS

Trusting a low-entropy output as "probably right"

Section 09's matrix shows the single worst cross-entropy value sitting exactly in a low-entropy (confident) row — sharpness and correctness are entirely independent facts.

ASSUMING SYMMETRY

Treating H(P,Q) as equal to H(Q,P)

Cross-entropy is directional — swapping which distribution is "true" and which is "predicted" generally changes the value, unlike a real distance.

UNGUARDED LOG(0)

A correct candidate's probability underflowing to exactly 0

At a very low temperature, floating-point can round a tiny q_correct all the way to 0, and −ln(0) is undefined — production code needs a small epsilon floor, not a bare log call.

NOISY LABELS

Treating a mislabeled "truth" as real signal

Cross-entropy against an incorrect label penalizes the model for being right — a data-quality problem cross-entropy cannot distinguish from a genuine mistake on its own.

CORRECTION

Misconceptions

Incorrect beliefCorrection
A distribution with low entropy is a good predictionFalse — low entropy only means concentrated, not correct; Section 09's matrix shows the worst cross-entropy value in a low-entropy row.
Cross-entropy and KL divergence are always the same numberTrue only when the true distribution's own entropy is 0 — the one-hot case this module always uses, per Section 09's identity; in general H(P,Q) = H(P) + KL(P‖Q).
Cross-entropy can be negative for a very confident, correct predictionFalse — −ln(x) for x ∈ (0,1] is always ≥ 0; the best possible cross-entropy is 0, approached only as q_correct → 1.
18
REALITY

Production implications

  • Compute log-softmax directly, not softmax followed by a separate log — fusing the two (as PyTorch's CrossEntropyLoss and log_softmax do) reuses Concept 06's log-sum-exp stability trick instead of losing precision twice.
  • Track entropy of a production retrieval system's score distributions over time as a monitoring signal — a sudden, sustained rise can indicate the system is losing confidence across the board, worth flagging even without a ground-truth label to compute cross-entropy against.
  • Guard against −ln(0) explicitly wherever a real training pipeline computes cross-entropy on a live model's outputs — an unguarded call can silently produce NaN losses the moment any predicted probability underflows to exactly 0.
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

Why does entropy never depend on which candidate is actually correct?
Entropy, H(P) = −Σpᵢln(pᵢ), is a function of the distribution's own probabilities alone — no true-label term appears anywhere in the formula (Section 05).
A distribution has low entropy. Does that guarantee low cross-entropy against any given true label? Why or why not?
No — Section 09's matrix shows a low-entropy (confident) distribution can produce the single highest cross-entropy on the whole map, if its confidence happens to point at the wrong candidate.
Why does cross-entropy against a one-hot truth equal KL divergence exactly?
H(P,Q) = H(P) + KL(P‖Q) always (Section 09); when P is one-hot, H(P) = 0, so the identity collapses to H(P,Q) = KL(P‖Q).

Solution and teach-back

Open the worked solution
Entropy: −(0.50·ln0.50 + 0.30·ln0.30 + 0.15·ln0.15 + 0.05·ln0.05) = −(0.50×−0.6931 + 0.30×−1.2040 + 0.15×−1.8971 + 0.05×−2.9957) = 0.3466+0.3612+0.2846+0.1498 ≈ 1.1421 nats. Both computed cross-entropy values equal their own KL divergence exactly, since the true distribution is one-hot in both cases (H(one-hot)=0, so H(P,Q)=KL(P‖Q) either way, per Section 09) — the question isn't which one KL equals, it's that KL always equals cross-entropy here, regardless of which candidate is marked true.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Explain, precisely, the difference between entropy and cross-entropy.
Entropy, H(P), takes one distribution and measures how spread out it is. Cross-entropy, H(P,Q), takes two — a true distribution P and a predicted one Q — and measures how much extra "surprise" using Q instead of P actually costs, on average under P (Section 05, Section 09).
Derive why cross-entropy against a one-hot true label reduces to −ln(q_correct).
H(P,Q) = −Σᵢ pᵢ ln(qᵢ). If P is one-hot with a 1 at index c and 0 elsewhere, every term with pᵢ=0 vanishes (by the 0·ln(0)=0 convention, and 0·ln(qᵢ)=0 for any qᵢ>0), leaving only −1·ln(q_c) = −ln(q_correct) (Section 09).
Debugging: a training run's cross-entropy loss occasionally spikes to NaN. What's the most likely cause, and the fix?
A predicted probability for the correct class underflowed to exactly 0 in floating-point, and −ln(0) is undefined — the fix is a numerically stable, fused log-softmax-plus-cross-entropy implementation (Section 13) rather than computing softmax and log as two separate steps, plus an epsilon floor as a last resort.
System design: you can only log one of {entropy, cross-entropy} per production query, with no ground-truth label available at serving time. Which one, and why?
Entropy — it requires no true label at all, so it's the only one of the two computable at serving time; cross-entropy needs a known-correct answer, which is exactly what's typically missing until an offline evaluation pass runs later.
