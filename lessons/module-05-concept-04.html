MODULE 05 · CONCEPT 04

How often here.
How rare everywhere.

Concept 03 named sparse and dense retrieval as two separate families but gave neither one a score — Concept 02's postings lists could only say a document contains a term, never how strongly. This concept gives the sparse mechanism its first two real numbers, introduced one at a time and kept deliberately separate: term frequency (TF) counts how many times a term appears inside one specific document — a local, per-document count. Inverse document frequency (IDF) measures how rare a term is across the whole corpus — a single, corpus-wide number attached to the term itself, identical no matter which document you look at. Neither number is combined with the other yet — Concept 05 multiplies them together into the TF-IDF formula.

4 CONCEPTS COMPLETED

PROGRESSIVE BEGINNER CORE

One count per document. One number per term.

Every idea in this concept reduces to a single question: does this number describe one document, or does it describe a term across the whole corpus? TF answers the first question. IDF answers the second. Keeping that scope straight is the entire lesson.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Two words for two different questions

TERM FREQUENCY (TF)

How many times does this term appear inside this one document? A per-document count — it changes from document to document, even for the same term.

INVERSE DOCUMENT FREQUENCY (IDF)

How rare is this term across the entire corpus? A single per-term number, computed once for the whole corpus, identical no matter which document is being scored.

First memory: TF asks "how much, here." IDF asks "how rare, everywhere." Different scope, different question, both about the same term.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Concept 02's postings lists could only say "yes" or "no"

Concept 02's inverted index tells you which documents contain a term — a membership fact, nothing more. Concept 03 named sparse retrieval as a family without giving it any way to prefer one matching document over another. This concept exists to attach the first two real numbers to that same sparse mechanism: how strongly a term shows up in one document, and how much information that term actually carries across the corpus as a whole. Neither number ranks anything by itself yet — Concept 05 is where they finally combine into a single score.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Five support tickets, one term at a time

A small corpus of five support tickets (N = 5):

T1

"my order is late and my order never arrived"

T2

"please refund my order immediately"

T3

"the refund was processed but the refund amount is wrong"

T4

"where is my order confirmation email"

T5

"i need help resetting my password"

Term frequency, per document, for "order": T1 = 2, T2 = 1, T3 = 0, T4 = 1, T5 = 0 — five different numbers for one term, because TF is local to each document. Inverse document frequency, for the whole corpus, for "order": 3 of the 5 tickets contain it at all, so idf("order") = ln(5/3) ≈ 0.51 — one number, and it would stay exactly 0.51 whether you're scoring T1, T4, or even T3, which doesn't contain "order" at all.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Counting inside one document, counting across all of them

TERM FREQUENCY

tf(t, d) — tokenize document d, then count how many of its tokens equal term t. A plain occurrence count, nothing more: no ranking, no comparison to any other document yet.

DOCUMENT FREQUENCY

df(t) — the number of documents in the corpus containing t at least once. This is exactly the length of Concept 02's postings list for t: no new counting mechanism needed, the inverted index already has this number sitting in it.

INVERSE DOCUMENT FREQUENCY

idf(t) = log(N / df(t)), where N is the total number of documents. Dividing by df and then taking a log turns "appears in many documents" into "small number" and "appears in few documents" into "large number" — the inverse relationship the name promises.

WHAT'S NOT HAPPENING YET

tf and idf are not multiplied together, added, or compared to each other anywhere in this concept. Concept 05 is the first place that combination happens.

05 · ARCHITECTURE / FLOW

Two new numbers, still inside the same box

Documents↓Chunks↓Inverted index — postings now carry a per-document TF count; each term also carries one corpus-wide IDF value↓Retrieval↓Context↓LLM↓Answer

Nothing moves to a new box. Concept 02's inverted index simply gets richer: instead of a postings list that only records which documents contain a term, each posting can now also record how many times, and the term itself can carry a single corpus-wide rarity value alongside it.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Four pairs this concept depends on keeping separate

TermIs NOTWhy it matters
Term frequency (tf)Document frequency (df)tf counts occurrences of a term inside one document; df counts how many documents contain that term at all, at least once. "Order" occurring twice in T1 is a tf fact about T1 alone; "order" occurring in 3 of 5 tickets is a df fact about the whole corpus.
Inverse document frequency (idf)A property of one documentidf is computed once per term, from N and df alone — it never looks at any single document's content, and it comes out identical for every document in the corpus.
A raw occurrence countA rankingKnowing tf("order", T1) = 2 says nothing yet about whether T1 outranks T2 — Concepts 04-06 build the ranking on top of these raw numbers, they aren't a ranking by themselves.
This concept's two separate numbersConcept 05's TF-IDF formulaThis concept computes tf and idf independently and never multiplies them. Concept 05 specifically is where tf(t,d) × idf(t) becomes one combined weight.
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"A term appearing twice must mean the document is twice as relevant."

It might just mean the document is longer, or repeats itself stylistically. Raw tf treats every document as if length didn't matter — Concept 07's document-length normalization is what eventually corrects for that, not this concept.

"IDF is a property of a document."

It's a property of a term, computed once from the whole corpus. Every document gets the exact same idf value for the same term — only tf varies by document.

"You can rank documents using idf alone."

You can't — idf doesn't vary across documents for a fixed term, so sorting documents by idf alone produces no differentiation between them at all. Ranking needs a per-document number (tf) too.

"A term with document frequency zero just gets a very high idf."

It gets an undefined one — log(N / 0) is a division by zero before the log even runs. Section 16 covers this failure mode directly.

In a ticket corpus like b03's, a rare term like "password" separates one ticket from the rest far more sharply than a common word like "my" ever could — the direct intuition idf is built to capture.

09 · WHAT YOU SHOULD UNDERSTAND

Readiness check before moving on

Term frequency

tf(t, d) = how many times term t appears inside document d. A per-document count, changes document to document.

Inverse document frequency

idf(t) = log(N / df(t)). A single per-term number, shared by every document in the corpus.

Why they're kept separate here

Neither number ranks anything alone; combining them is Concept 05's job specifically, not this one's.

The edge case to remember

df(t) = 0 makes idf undefined (division by zero before the log). df(t) = N makes idf exactly zero.

Teach-back questions

  1. In your own words, why does it make sense that tf can differ across all five tickets in b03, while idf for that same term cannot?
  2. Using b03's numbers, which single ticket would "password" single out most sharply, and why does its low document frequency make that possible?
  3. If a hypothetical term appeared in all 5 of b03's tickets, what would its idf equal, and what does that value say about how useful the term is for telling tickets apart?

04
INTUITION

A word count inside one essay, versus how many essays use that word at all

Term frequency behaves like counting how many times a specific word shows up inside one essay you're holding — a fact about that essay alone, unrelated to any other essay in the stack. Inverse document frequency behaves like asking, across an entire stack of essays, how many of them use that word at all — if nearly every essay in the stack says "the," that word tells you nothing about which essay is about what; if only one essay says "photosynthesis," that word is a strong clue pointing straight at that one essay.

Analogy boundaryThe essay-stack analogy explains why rarity carries information, but it doesn't explain the exact formula — log(N / df) specifically, not some other rarity measure — that's Section 05 and Section 09's job, not the intuition's.

PRECISION

Formal definitions

TermDefinitionNotation
Corpus sizeTotal number of documents in the corpusN = |corpus|
Term frequencyNumber of times term t occurs inside document dtf(t, d), a non-negative integer, one value per (term, document) pair
Document frequencyNumber of documents containing t at least oncedf(t) = |{ d ∈ corpus : tf(t, d) > 0 }|, equal to Concept 02's postings-list length for t

06
MECHANISM

Counting inside one document, then counting across all of them

Corpus arrives — five support tickets, T1 through T5, N = 5 (b03's exact text).
Tokenize each document — split each ticket's text into individual terms, exactly as Concept 02 already does to build postings lists.
Count occurrences per document (tf) — for one chosen term, count how many of that document's tokens equal it. Repeat separately for every document — five separate counts.
Count documents containing the term (df) — instead of counting occurrences, count how many documents have at least one occurrence. This is just the length of the term's postings list.
Divide and take a log (idf) — idf(t) = log(N / df(t)) — one division, one log, done once per term, not once per document.
07
VISUALIZATION

One term, two branches, two different outputs

Term + corpusidentical input to both computations
TF pathtokenize document → count occurrences → one number per document
IDF pathcount containing documents (df) → divide into N → log → one number per term

Both branches start from the same term and the same corpus, but they produce outputs of a completely different shape — a list of per-document counts on one side, a single scalar on the other. Concept 05 is the first place those two differently-shaped outputs get combined, once per (term, document) pair.

08
ARCHITECTURE

Where both numbers sit in the retrieval-to-answer chain

Documents↓Chunks↓Inverted index — postings carry per-document TF; each term carries one corpus-wide IDF↓Retrieval↓Context↓LLM↓Answer

The "Retrieval" box's sparse path hasn't gained a new stage — Concept 02's inverted index is simply carrying two more numbers than it did before: a count alongside each posting, and a rarity value alongside each term.

09
MATHEMATICS

Two formulas, still no multiplication between them

tf(t, d) = count of term t among document d's tokens -- one integer per (term, document) pair df(t) = |{ d in corpus : tf(t, d) > 0 }| -- Concept 02's postings-list length for t idf(t) = log( N / df(t) ) -- one real number per term, shared by every document

The log base is a free choice — natural log, log base 10, log base 2 — and it doesn't change which term ranks as rarer than another, because log is monotonically increasing (Module 04, Concept 06 already established this exact fact for softmax scores; it applies identically here). This lesson uses natural log (ln) throughout its worked numbers. Two boundary values worth memorizing: if df(t) = N (the term is in every document), idf(t) = log(1) = 0 exactly — zero weight. If df(t) = 0 (the term is in no document), N / df(t) is a division by zero and idf is undefined, not simply "very large."

10
WORKED EXAMPLE

"refund," computed completely by hand

Using b03's five tickets: tf("refund", T1) = 0, tf("refund", T2) = 1, tf("refund", T3) = 2, tf("refund", T4) = 0, tf("refund", T5) = 0.

StepQuantityValueHow it was found
1df("refund")2Two tickets have tf > 0 for "refund": T2 and T3.
2N5Total tickets in the corpus.
3N / df2.55 ÷ 2.
4idf("refund")≈ 0.9163ln(2.5), independently recomputed for this lesson.

Notice tf("refund", T3) = 2 is the single largest per-document count in this example, while idf("refund") ≈ 0.9163 is one fixed number that would apply equally to T1, T4, or T5 even though none of them contain "refund" at all. That asymmetry — a rich per-document signal next to a flat per-term one — is exactly what Concept 05 combines.

11
CONCRETE USE

Two clean fits, a boundary case, a mistake

CLEAN FIT — TF

A long support thread that keeps repeating "refund"

A ticket that says "refund" five times across a long back-and-forth is plausibly more about a refund than one that mentions it once in passing — tf captures that intuition directly.

CLEAN FIT — IDF

Distinguishing "password" from "my" in b03's corpus

"my" appears in 4 of 5 tickets and carries almost no discriminating power; "password" appears in exactly 1 and immediately points at a single ticket — idf quantifies that gap precisely.

BOUNDARY CASE

A term present in literally every document

If df(t) = N, idf(t) = log(N/N) = log(1) = 0 exactly — a term that appears everywhere gets exactly zero weight once Concept 05 multiplies it against tf, no matter how large tf is for any one document.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Comparing raw tf across two documents of very different length

tf("order", T1) = 2 versus tf("order", T4) = 1 looks like T1 cares more about orders — but that comparison ignores document length entirely, which is precisely why Concept 07 exists as its own concept rather than being folded in here.

12
CODE

Term frequency and inverse document frequency, from scratch

PYTHON · NO LIBRARIES 
import math

tickets = {
    "T1": "my order is late and my order never arrived",
    "T2": "please refund my order immediately",
    "T3": "the refund was processed but the refund amount is wrong",
    "T4": "where is my order confirmation email",
    "T5": "i need help resetting my password",
}

def term_frequency(term, text):
    return text.split(" ").count(term)

def document_frequency(term, corpus):
    return sum(1 for text in corpus.values() if term in text.split(" "))

def inverse_document_frequency(term, corpus):
    df = document_frequency(term, corpus)
    if df == 0:
        return None  # undefined: N / 0 before the log even runs -- guard, don't crash
    return math.log(len(corpus) / df)

# tf varies document by document, even for the same term.
assert term_frequency("order", tickets["T1"]) == 2
assert term_frequency("order", tickets["T2"]) == 1
assert term_frequency("order", tickets["T3"]) == 0

# df and idf are single numbers describing the whole corpus.
assert document_frequency("refund", tickets) == 2
idf_refund = inverse_document_frequency("refund", tickets)
assert abs(idf_refund - 0.9163) < 0.001          # matches Section 10's hand computation

# Edge case: a term absent from every document has no defined idf here.
assert inverse_document_frequency("shipping", tickets) is None

# Boundary case: a term present in every document gets exactly zero weight.
assert math.log(5 / 5) == 0.0
13
ECOSYSTEM

Where production systems keep these exact two numbers

Apache Lucene (and Elasticsearch/OpenSearch on top of it) stores per-term document frequency directly inside its index structures and computes idf from it at query time as one component of its scoring function. Scikit-learn's TfidfVectorizer computes term frequency and document frequency the same way this concept does, then applies a smoothed variant of idf — commonly log((1+N)/(1+df)) + 1 — specifically to avoid the df = 0 undefined case Section 11 and Section 16 both flag; that smoothing detail is a real-world refinement worth knowing exists, not a formula this concept derives.

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

UNGUARDED DIVISION

Computing idf for a term with df = 0

N / 0 is undefined before the log even runs. Section 12's code guards against this explicitly; unguarded code either crashes or silently returns infinity, which is not a usable score.

LENGTH BLINDNESS

Treating tf as fair across documents of different length

A longer document naturally accumulates higher raw counts for almost every term, independent of relevance — Concept 07's document-length normalization exists specifically to correct this, not this concept.

RANKING BY IDF ALONE

Trying to sort documents using only idf

Idf is identical for every document containing (or not containing) a fixed term — sorting by it alone produces ties across every document that shares that property, because it isn't a per-document number at all.

17
CORRECTION

Misconceptions

Incorrect beliefCorrection
A term appearing twice in a document is twice as relevant as one appearing onceFalse — tf is a raw count with no built-in notion of relevance or document length; it's an input to a later score, not a score itself.
Idf changes depending on which document you're scoringFalse — idf is computed once per term from N and df alone; it is the same number for every document in the corpus.
A rare term automatically has a very high, well-defined idfFalse in the extreme case — a term with df = 0 (absent from every document) makes idf undefined, not simply large; Section 13's smoothed variants exist to handle this in practice.
18
REALITY

Production implications

  • Term frequency is essentially free — it falls out of the same tokenization pass that already builds Concept 02's postings lists, with no extra corpus-wide scan required.
  • Document frequency and idf require knowing the corpus size N and every term's df, which means adding, removing, or editing even one document shifts every existing term's idf slightly — an index-wide recomputation or incremental-update concern (Module 19, forward reference), not a one-document update.
  • Storing a per-term idf value alongside a vocabulary of potentially millions of terms is cheap compared to storing per-document tf counts across the whole corpus — a real memory-versus-freshness trade-off production systems have to manage explicitly.
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

What is the difference between document frequency and term frequency?
Term frequency counts how many times a term occurs inside one specific document. Document frequency counts how many documents in the corpus contain that term at all, regardless of how many times.
Why is idf the same value for every document in the corpus, for a fixed term?
Because idf is computed only from N (corpus size) and df(t) (how many documents contain t) — it never inspects the content of any single document, so it can't vary from one document to the next.
What happens to idf if a term appears in every single document?
df(t) = N, so idf(t) = log(N/N) = log(1) = 0 exactly — the term gets zero weight once combined with tf in Concept 05.

FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open the worked solution
tf("is", T1) = 1, tf("is", T4) = 1, tf("is", T2) = tf("is", T3) = tf("is", T5) = 0. df("is") = 2 (T1 and T4). 23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Explain term frequency and inverse document frequency to someone who has never heard either term, in under thirty seconds.
Term frequency is how many times a word shows up in one document you're looking at. Inverse document frequency is how rare that word is across every document you have — common words score low, rare words score high, and that rarity score is the same no matter which document you check it against.
Why can't you rank documents using inverse document frequency alone?
Idf doesn't vary by document for a fixed term — it's one shared number. Ranking requires something that differs per document, which is exactly what term frequency provides.
What breaks if you compute idf for a term that appears in zero documents, and how would you guard against it in code?
N divided by a document frequency of zero is undefined before the log function even runs. Guard by checking df == 0 first and returning a sentinel (or skipping the term entirely) rather than letting the division execute.
Design question: a teammate proposes ranking retrieved documents by idf sum alone, without ever touching term frequency. What's wrong with that plan?
Idf sum over the query terms would be identical for every document containing those terms — it carries no information about how strongly any particular document actually uses them. Without tf, every document that merely contains the terms ties for the same score, defeating the purpose of ranking at all.
