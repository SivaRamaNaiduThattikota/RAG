01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Split into pieces, then turn each piece into a vector

Document chunking and embedding flow is the pipeline that turns one document into several pieces (chunks), then turns each piece into a dense vector (an embedding) that captures its meaning. Two separate stages, two separate failure modes -- a bad split can't be fixed by a good embedder, and a diluted pool can't be fixed by a perfect split.

First memory: chunking decides what text goes into a vector; embedding decides how that text becomes a vector.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

A whole document is too big and too diluted to embed as one vector

A whole document embedded as a single vector dilutes its own meaning across everything it contains (Module 07 Concept 01's own dilution finding), and retrieval needs to compare at roughly query-length granularity, not document-length. The document has to be split first, then each piece embedded separately -- that's the entire reason this flow exists.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

One fact, two overlap sizes, two different outcomes

Take a fresh passage: "Oracle Order Management lets a customer cancel a shipped order within a grace period. The grace period is fourteen days from the delivery date, not the order date. After fourteen days, the system blocks self-service cancellation and routes the request to a support agent instead." (279 characters, 45 words). The fact to protect -- "fourteen days from the delivery date, not the order date" -- is 10 words long. Split into 16-word chunks with only 6 words of overlap, that fact survives intact in 0 of 4 chunks. Split again with 10 words of overlap (exactly the fact's own length), it survives intact in 2 of 6 chunks.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Split, then four sub-steps to embed

Split -- word-based chunking at a fixed size, with overlap sized relative to the fact you're protecting. Tokenize -- the chunk text (prefixed "passage: ", this course's own adopted convention) is broken into subword tokens. Look up token embeddings -- each token gets an initial vector. Contextualize -- bidirectional attention updates every token's vector using every other token in the chunk. Pool -- attention-mask-weighted mean pooling averages all token vectors into one 384-dimensional chunk vector, then L2-normalized.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Overlap sized right guarantees a floor, not a ceiling

overlap_size ≥ fact_length guarantees at least one chunk boundary the fact doesn't cross -- it does not guarantee the fact survives in every window, and it does not guarantee most windows contain it. It's a necessary floor, not a full guarantee: this concept's own 2-of-6 result at exactly the fact's length proves the floor is real but modest.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"Overlap below the fact's length is close enough."

False -- this concept's own worked example shows overlap=6 against a 10-word fact keeps it intact in exactly 0 of 4 chunks, not "mostly."

"A better embedding model can fix a bad split."

False -- if the fact never appears intact in any chunk, no embedding model, however good, has any chunk vector to represent it faithfully. The split has to happen right first.

06
INTUITION

A chunk boundary is a bet

Drawing a chunk boundary is a bet that no single fact spans it. Overlap is how you hedge that bet without re-embedding the whole document at every possible offset -- it doesn't remove the bet, it just narrows how badly it can go wrong.

Analogy boundaryThe betting picture explains why overlap exists, not the exact guarantee -- that comes from Section 10's own inequality, not from the analogy.
07
INTUITION

Pooling is compression, not labeling

Embedding a chunk isn't attaching a label to it -- it's compressing every token's contextualized meaning into one fixed-size average. What survives that average depends entirely on which tokens dominate it; a chunk full of function words dilutes its own content words the same way a whole document dilutes its own facts.

08
INTUITION

The two stages compound

A bad split (a fact straddling a boundary) can't be rescued by a good embedder -- there's no intact chunk left to embed well. A diluted pool (too many function words drowning the content) can't be rescued by a perfect split either. Both stages have to work.

09
MECHANISM

Splitting mechanics

Word-based chunking at a fixed size of 16 words, with stride = chunk_size − overlap (Module 07 Concept 06). At overlap=6, stride=10, producing 4 chunks from the 45-word passage. At overlap=10, stride=6, producing 6 chunks from the same passage.

10
MATHEMATICS

The overlap-sizing guarantee, restated precisely

overlap_size ≥ fact_length ⟹ at least one chunk boundary the fact doesn't cross exists
overlap_size < fact_length ⟹ every stride position can split the fact

This is a floor, not a ceiling -- it guarantees the fact survives intact somewhere, not that most or all windows contain it.

11
MECHANISM

Tokenization, and the prefix convention

Chunk text is prefixed "passage: " + chunk_text before tokenization -- this course's own adopted convention from the E5/BGE family (Module 08 Concept 03, Concept 02's own reuse in this module), not an architectural requirement of all-MiniLM-L6-v2 itself. Stating this explicitly so it isn't mistaken for a MiniLM feature.

12
MECHANISM

Contextualization

Bidirectional attention ("Attention Is All You Need", BERT) updates every token's vector using every other token in the chunk, not just left-context -- each token's contextualized vector reflects the whole chunk, not just what preceded it.

13
MECHANISM

Pooling

Attention-mask-weighted mean pooling (Sentence-BERT) averages every contextualized token vector into one fixed-size chunk vector, then L2-normalizes it so a dot product between two chunk vectors equals their cosine similarity.

16
CODE

Pooling, verified against the real library

PYTHON · MANUAL MEAN-POOL + L2-NORMALIZE, vs. model.encode() 
outputs = model(**inputs)
token_vecs = outputs.last_hidden_state  # shape: (23, 384)
mask = inputs["attention_mask"]
pooled = (token_vecs * mask.unsqueeze(-1)).sum(1) / mask.sum(1, keepdim=True)
manual = pooled / pooled.norm(dim=1, keepdim=True)  # L2-normalize

library = model.encode([prefixed_text], normalize_embeddings=True)

cosine(manual, library)  # 1.0000001 (float32 noise)
max_abs_diff(manual, library)  # 1.04e-07

WORKED EXAMPLE

The survival result, run against real model weights

Verification methodRun locally against the actual sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2 weights -- not a re-implementation. Both the chunk-boundary logic and the embedding numbers are exact, from a real forward pass, not approximated.

overlap=6 (below the 10-word fact) keeps the fact intact in exactly 0 of 4 chunks -- it splits across a boundary at every stride position. overlap=10 (exactly at the fact's length) keeps it intact in 2 of 6 chunks -- proof that the guarantee is a real floor, not a ceiling: most windows still miss it even when the guarantee holds.

18
EVIDENCE + CONTROL

Control check -- manual pooling matches the library exactly

Manual pooling vs. the library's own model.encode(): cosine similarity 1.0000001 (1.0 within float32 noise), maximum absolute per-dimension difference 1.04e-7. This confirms the hand-traced pooling math above matches the real implementation exactly -- "pool" stops being a black box.

CONCRETE USE

Reading the lab's token-weight bar

At the pool stage, high-weight content tokens ("customs", "clearance") dominate the mean; low-weight function tokens ("the", "an") dilute it -- the same dilution mechanism Module 07 Concept 01 already named at the whole-document scale, now visible at the single-chunk scale.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Given a fact's length, a chunk size, and an overlap value, how do you predict whether the fact survives intact in some chunk?
Compute overlap_size vs. fact_length: if overlap is at or above the fact's length, at least one intact chunk is guaranteed to exist; below that, every stride position can split the fact.
Why can't a better embedding model fix a bad chunk split?
If the fact never appears intact in any chunk, there's no chunk vector that could faithfully represent it -- the embedding stage only ever sees what the split stage handed it.
