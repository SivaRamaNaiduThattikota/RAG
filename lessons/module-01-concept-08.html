01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Capability, limitation, and responsible use

A capability is something a model does reliably well across many inputs—for example, producing fluent, well-formed language and adapting style to instructions. A limitation is a way it reliably falls short—for example, stating outdated or invented facts with the same fluent confidence as true ones.

Responsible use means matching each task to a method that respects that boundary: raw generation where fluency is the goal, retrieval or tools where exactness and currency matter, and human review where the cost of being wrong is high.

First memory: a fluent answer and a correct answer are not the same claim.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Why capability limits matter now, before Module 02

Concepts 4–7 explained a model that predicts plausible next tokens from learned patterns and a fixed set of parameters. Nothing in that mechanism checks a generated claim against a current, authoritative source. Naming these limits precisely is what motivates retrieval-augmented generation, which the rest of this course builds—RAG is not a decoration on top of a generative model, it is a direct response to this boundary.

WHAT GENERATION ALONE DOES WELL

Fluent language, style adaptation, broad general knowledge patterns, drafting and summarizing.

WHAT IT DOES NOT GUARANTEE

Current facts, exact private records, verifiable citations, or safe high-stakes decisions.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Three requests, three very different risks

“Draft a friendly reminder about the leave policy” is low-stakes and stylistic—raw generation is appropriate. “What is my current leave balance?” is an exact, private, frequently changing fact—raw generation can produce a fluent, wrong number with no warning sign. “Should I approve this leave request given labor law?” carries real consequences—it needs sourced evidence and human judgment, not a confident-sounding paragraph.

STYLE / DRAFTING

Safe for direct generation.

EXACT / CURRENT FACT

Needs retrieval or a database lookup, not memory.

HIGH-STAKES DECISION

Needs sourced evidence plus human review.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Where the limitations actually come from

HALLUCINATION

Fluent but unsupported

The decoding loop always produces a plausible-sounding token sequence, whether or not it is true.

KNOWLEDGE CUTOFF

Frozen at training time

Parametric knowledge cannot reflect events or records created after training data was collected.

NO BUILT-IN VERIFICATION

No source-checking step

The model does not consult an external source unless a system explicitly retrieves and provides one.

DISTRIBUTIONAL BIAS

Reflects training data patterns

Skewed or unbalanced patterns in training data can surface as skewed outputs.

REASONING LIMITS

Pattern completion, not proof

Multi-step reasoning can fail silently, producing a confident but invalid chain.

PRIVACY EXPOSURE

Provider and logging boundaries

Sending private data to a model or provider carries its own governance requirements.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Failure types that get lumped together

Failure What actually happens Fixed by
Hallucination Fluent claim with no supporting evidence Grounding, retrieval, citation checking
Knowledge cutoff Correct as of training time, now stale Retrieval over current sources
Bias Skewed patterns from training data Evaluation, data review, guardrails
Non-determinism Different answers under sampling (Concept 7) Decoding-parameter control, not grounding

Only one of these—non-determinism—is a decoding choice from Concept 7. The rest are limits of what the model’s parameters and training data can guarantee, and no decoding setting fixes them.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often misunderstand

“A confident tone means a correct answer.”

Fluency and confidence are properties of generation, not evidence of truth.

“Greedy decoding removes hallucination.”

It removes randomness, not the lack of a verification step.

“A bigger model has no knowledge cutoff.”

Every model’s parametric knowledge is frozen at its training data’s collection date.

“If it cites a source, the source is real.”

A generated citation is still generated text unless it was actually retrieved and checked.

“Responsible use is only a legal concern.”

It also covers accuracy, privacy, and matching method to stakes—an engineering decision.

“RAG alone guarantees correctness.”

It reduces knowledge-cutoff and grounding risk; it does not eliminate every failure mode.

08 · YOUR PROJECT MAPPING

Why Oracle RAG exists, stated precisely

POLICY EXPLANATION Stable, public-ish → retrieval + generation

Generation drafts a recommendation; a person remains accountable for the decision.

CASUAL DRAFTING Low-stakes → direct generation

Reminders, summaries, and rephrasing rarely need retrieval.

Every module after this one narrows this same judgment: which parts of an answer must be grounded, how grounding is measured, and how failures are caught before a user sees them.

DEPENDENCIES

  • Inference: using fixed parameters on new input, with no external verification by default (Concept 5).
04
INTUITION

A fluent narrator, not a sworn witness

A model is like a fluent narrator retelling a story from memory: confident, well-phrased, and sometimes simply wrong about a detail it never actually verified. A sworn witness is expected to say “I don’t know” or check a record; a narrator, by default, keeps talking.

Analogy boundaryThe model has no intent to mislead—its training objective never included a “verify against a source” step, so fluent and false outputs are produced by the exact same mechanism as fluent and true ones.
05
PRECISION

Definitions by failure type

Term Definition Primary cause
Hallucination Fluent output not supported by any verifiable source No verification step in generation
Knowledge cutoff Parametric knowledge frozen at training data collection time Static training corpus
Bias Systematic skew in outputs reflecting training-data patterns Imbalanced or unrepresentative training data
Reasoning failure An invalid multi-step chain produced with fluent confidence Pattern completion without guaranteed logical validity
06
MECHANISM

Why the same loop produces both true and false fluent text

Prompt tokenized into model input
Model predicts a next-token distribution from learned patterns
Decoding selects a token—true or false content scores no differently here
Token appended; loop repeats until a stop condition
Output is fluent by construction; correctness was never checked

Nothing in Concepts 4–7’s mechanism includes a step that consults an external, current, authoritative source. That step has to be added deliberately—it is what retrieval, tools, and evaluation exist to provide.

  Low stakes if wrong High stakes if wrong

Most real tasks are not identical to one of Concepts 1–7’s clean examples—they sit somewhere in this grid, and the grid, not model confidence, is what should decide the method.

09
MATHEMATICS

Confidence is not calibrated to correctness

A model’s token-level probability P(token_i) reflects how expected that token is given training patterns and context, not the probability that the resulting claim is factually true. Two sentences can receive similar decoding probability while one is verifiably correct and the other is invented—probability here is a fluency signal, not a truth signal.

P(token sequence) ≠ P(claim is true)
10
WORKED EXAMPLE

Two equally fluent answers, one false

Asked “How many paid leave days does the policy allow?”, a model with stale or unclear parametric knowledge might generate “12 days” and “15 days” across two calls—both fluent, both grammatically identical in confidence, but the approved policy document states 10. Neither generated number carried any signal distinguishing it from the correct one; only checking against the actual document resolves this.

12
CODE

A minimal task router

PYTHON · FROM-SCRATCH PSEUDOCODE 
def route_task(is_exact_or_current, is_high_stakes):
    if is_exact_or_current:
        method = "retrieval_or_database"
    else:
        method = "direct_generation"
    review = "human_review_required" if is_high_stakes else "light_review"
    return method, review

route_task(is_exact_or_current=True, is_high_stakes=True)
# -> ("retrieval_or_database", "human_review_required")

Mitigations and what they actually fix

Mitigation Fixes Does not fix

DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

SILENT HALLUCINATION

No hedge, no warning

An invented detail delivered with the same fluency as a verified one.

OVER-TRUST

Treating fluency as proof

Users or systems act on generated text without checking it against a source.

STALE CONFIDENCE

Outdated fact stated plainly

A once-true answer stated as current without a currency check.

MISROUTED TASK

Wrong method for the stakes

A high-stakes task handled with plain generation and no review.

UNREVIEWED BIAS

Skew reaches production

No evaluation step catches a systematic pattern before users see it.

PRIVACY LEAK

Sensitive data sent upstream

Private records passed to a model or provider without governance review.

17
CORRECTION

Misconceptions

Incorrect belief Correction
A more fluent answer is more likely correct Fluency is a property of decoding, unrelated to factual verification.
Only small or old models hallucinate Every generative model can produce unsupported fluent claims without grounding.
Responsible use is a one-time policy document It is an ongoing routing and review practice applied per task.
Grounded answers never need review Retrieval reduces knowledge-cutoff risk; it does not remove the need for judgment on high-stakes tasks.
18
REALITY

Production implications

  • Classify tasks by stakes and currency before choosing generation, retrieval, or both.
  • Log generated claims that influenced a real decision, with enough context to audit later.
  • Keep a human accountable for high-stakes actions even when evidence is well-grounded.
  • Treat privacy and data-handling review as part of shipping, not an afterthought.
  • Re-evaluate routing decisions as retrieval, tools, and evaluation capabilities mature in later modules.
  • Disclose AI-generated content where your organization’s policy or regulation requires it.
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

Why can two hallucinated numbers sound equally confident?
Decoding probability reflects fluency and pattern likelihood, not a check against a real source, so a false and a true continuation can appear equally natural.
What does retrieval fix, and what does it not fix?
It fixes missing or outdated evidence by supplying current, authoritative context; it does not fix reasoning errors or remove the need for human review on high-stakes tasks.
Why is “responsible use” an engineering decision, not only a policy one?
Because it requires actively routing tasks to generation, retrieval, or review based on stakes and currency—a design choice made in the system, not just a written guideline.
How does this concept motivate the rest of the course?
It names the exact gap—no built-in verification against current, authoritative evidence—that retrieval-augmented generation is built to close.

FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open one worked reasoning pattern
For each task, first ask whether the answer depends on an exact, current, or private fact—if so, that alone requires retrieval or a database, regardless of stakes. Then ask what happens if the answer is wrong—if the consequence is significant, add human review on top of any grounding. Tasks that are neither exact/current nor high-stakes are the only ones safe for plain generation with light review.

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

How would you explain hallucination to a non-technical stakeholder?
The model produces fluent, plausible text by pattern completion; nothing in that process checks the claim against a real source, so confident-sounding text can still be false.
Design a simple policy for routing tasks to generation versus RAG versus human review.
Classify by whether the answer depends on an exact/current/private fact (route to retrieval) and by the cost of being wrong (route to human review above a stakes threshold), defaulting to direct generation only when neither applies.
Does RAG eliminate hallucination?
No—it substantially reduces ungrounded claims by supplying evidence, but the model can still misstate or misinterpret that evidence, so evaluation and review remain necessary.
What is one privacy consideration specific to generative systems?
Data sent into a prompt or retained in logs may leave your governance boundary depending on the model provider and logging configuration, independent of what the model generates.
