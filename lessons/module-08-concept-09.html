MODULE 08 · CONCEPT 09

Not every model.
Speaks every language, or every domain.

Concepts 01-08 all implicitly assumed the embedding model is a general-purpose, English-trained encoder. This concept names the first real deviation from that assumption: multilingual embedding models, trained so semantically equivalent text in different languages lands in the SAME shared vector space -- no translation step required. A Spanish support query against an English knowledge-base document scores 0.999354 under a real multilingual training recipe, but -0.306189 -- negative, worse than unrelated -- under an English-only model that never learned to place Spanish text anywhere coherent. The second deviation is domain-specific models: fine-tuned on narrow-domain text so jargon like "novation" gets a real, learned position instead of collapsing toward an unrelated common-vocabulary neighbor. LaBSE's real numbers (83.7% vs. 65.5% Tatoeba bi-text retrieval across 112 languages) and MedCPT's (beating a GPT-3-sized model on biomedical retrieval) ground both claims in production evidence, not toy geometry alone.

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Two real deviations from "general-purpose English encoder"

MULTILINGUAL MODELS

Trained so semantically equivalent text in different languages lands in the SAME shared vector space -- no translation step required.

DOMAIN-SPECIFIC MODELS

Fine-tuned on narrow-domain text (legal, medical, code) so in-domain jargon gets a real, learned position instead of collapsing toward noise.

First memory: a model's training data has a language mix and a domain mix -- production text that doesn't match either one degrades retrieval, in two different ways.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Every real model was trained on SOME specific mix

No embedding model is trained on every language and every domain equally. When production text falls outside the mix a model actually learned, retrieval degrades -- multilingual mismatch and domain-jargon mismatch are the two concrete, well-documented ways this happens.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

A Spanish query, and a legal term, under two models each

Spanish query "¿cuánto cuesta el envío?" against an English document ("Standard shipping costs $5.99."): a multilingual model scores cosine 0.999354; an English-only model scores -0.306189 -- negative, worse than unrelated. Legal term "novation" against a true contract clause: a domain-tuned model scores 0.995797; a general model scores -0.003623 -- essentially orthogonal, meaning nothing.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Alignment training vs. no alignment training

Multilingual models like LaBSE are trained with objectives (masked language modeling, translation-language modeling, translation-ranking) that explicitly pull translation-equivalent sentences together in vector space. An English-only model was never shown that pull -- Spanish subwords combine into whatever direction the pooling arithmetic happens to produce, with no guarantee it relates to the English meaning at all.

05 · ARCHITECTURE / FLOW

A choice made once, offline, before any request

Choose model: general / multilingual / domain-tuned→ Every downstream embedding uses that one choice (this concept)→ Concept 10 — the actual selection framework, unopened
06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Alignment vs. translation, and a training-time vs. request-time choice

PairingThe real difference
Multilingual alignment vs. machine translationNo text is translated -- languages are geometrically aligned into one shared space
This concept vs. Concept 08's prefixA trained-weights choice made once, offline, vs. a literal string prepended every single request
"Trained on multiple languages" vs. "trained so languages share one space"Not automatically the same thing -- alignment is a deliberate training objective, not a side effect of exposure
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"A multilingual model is basically a built-in translator."

False -- no text is translated. The model geometrically aligns meanings into one shared space.

"A bigger general-purpose model always beats a smaller domain-tuned one."

False -- MedCPT beats a GPT-3-sized general model on biomedical retrieval.

"If the cosine score isn't literally negative, the mismatch is probably fine."

False -- MedCPT's real gain is comparative, not about catastrophic negative scores; a merely mediocre score can still mean a real, costly mismatch.

04
INTUITION

A bilingual interpreter vs. a monolingual reader

A bilingual interpreter recognizes that a Spanish sentence and its English paraphrase mean the same thing. A monolingual English reader confronted with Spanish text has no such recognition at all. A specialist reading a jargon-dense legal report understands "novation" precisely; a generalist may only recognize the surface-similar but unrelated word "innovation."

Analogy boundaryThe human picture explains WHY the contrast exists. It doesn't capture the actual MLM/TLM/contrastive-fine-tuning training mechanics -- Section 06 states those precisely.

06
MECHANISM

Alignment training, and in-domain contrastive fine-tuning

LaBSE's recipe combines masked language modeling, translation-language modeling, translation-ranking, and additive-margin softmax to explicitly pull translation-equivalent sentences together, cited at the recipe level, not re-derived. MedCPT and CodeRetriever both fine-tune on in-domain query-document pairs using a contrastive objective -- the same InfoNCE-style mechanism Concept 04 already named, just applied to biomedical or code pairs instead of general text.

09
MATHEMATICS

No new formula -- the same cosine, two new scenarios

cosine(a,b) = dot(a,b) / (‖a‖·‖b‖) — Module 04 Concept 03, cited unchanged

This concept introduces no new mathematics. It applies the identical cosine formula every prior Module 08 concept has used to two new scenarios where the underlying vectors themselves -- not the formula -- change with the model's training data.

10
WORKED EXAMPLE

Two scenarios, two models each

Part 1 -- cross-lingual scenario. Spanish query "¿cuánto cuesta el envío?" against English document "Standard shipping costs $5.99." D_doc = (0.62, 0.78) (same under either model, since it's English). Multilingual model: Q_multi = (0.65, 0.76) -- cos(Q_multi, D_doc) = 0.999354. English-only model (illustrative stand-in for untrained-language tokens combining incoherently): Q_mono = (0.20, -0.30) -- cos(Q_mono, D_doc) = -0.306189, negative, worse than unrelated.

Part 2 -- domain-jargon scenario. Term "novation" against a true contract-clause vector D_legal = (0.75, 0.45). Domain-tuned model: V_domain = (0.71, 0.52) -- cos(V_domain, D_legal) = 0.995797. General model (illustrative stand-in for falling back toward "novelty/innovation"): V_general = (-0.49, 0.81) -- cos(V_general, D_legal) = -0.003623, essentially orthogonal.

Part 3 -- real evidence. LaBSE: 83.7% bi-text retrieval accuracy across 112 languages on Tatoeba, versus 65.5% for the prior baseline (Artetxe & Schwenk 2019) -- a real +18.2 percentage-point jump. MedCPT: state-of-the-art on six biomedical IR tasks, beating "much larger models such as GPT-3-sized cpt-text-XL." BGE-M3 supports 100+ languages up to 8,192 tokens; CodeRetriever reaches new state-of-the-art across eleven domain/language-specific code-search tasks in six programming languages.

Verification method: all four cosines were computed via a cos(a,b)=dot(a,b)/(‖a‖‖b‖) helper function, then independently re-computed via separately-written manual dot()/norm() calls -- both passes produced identical results to 6 decimal places on every value.

11
CONCRETE USE

A clean fit, a boundary case, and a mistake

CLEAN FIT

A multilingual support-ticket RAG system

Users query in their own language against a knowledge base written in another.

BOUNDARY CASE

A bilingual corpus needing cross-lingual matching only sometimes

Most queries and documents already match language -- the alignment only matters for the minority that don't.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Embedding a jargon-heavy corpus with a general model

And assuming a returned, non-error cosine score means retrieval is fine.

12
CODE

The same cosine, reproducing both scenarios

PYTHON · REUSES MODULE 04 CONCEPT 03'S cosine() UNCHANGED 
import math

def dot(a, b):
    return a[0]*b[0] + a[1]*b[1]

def norm(a):
    return math.sqrt(dot(a, a))

def cosine(a, b):
    return dot(a, b) / (norm(a) * norm(b))

d_doc, q_multi, q_mono = (0.62,0.78), (0.65,0.76), (0.20,-0.30)
assert round(cosine(q_multi, d_doc), 6) == 0.999354
assert round(cosine(q_mono, d_doc), 6) == -0.306189

d_legal, v_domain, v_general = (0.75,0.45), (0.71,0.52), (-0.49,0.81)
assert round(cosine(v_domain, d_legal), 6) == 0.995797
assert round(cosine(v_general, d_legal), 6) == -0.003623

ECOSYSTEM

Four real, verified models

LaBSE (Feng, Yang, Cer et al.) achieves 83.7% bi-text retrieval accuracy across 112 languages on Tatoeba, versus 65.5% for the prior baseline, and is publicly released for 109+ languages. BGE-M3 (Chen, Xiao, Zhang et al.) provides uniform support for over 100 working languages and handles documents up to 8,192 tokens. MedCPT (Jin, Kim, Chen et al.), trained on 255 million PubMed user click logs, sets new state-of-the-art on six biomedical IR tasks, outperforming much larger models. CodeRetriever (Microsoft) learns function-level code semantic representations via large-scale contrastive pretraining, evaluated on eleven domain/language-specific code-search tasks across six programming languages.

21
FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open the worked solution
Both mismatches independently degrade retrieval, and neither one being present would still leave the other biting -- French-against-English (no shared space) risks a near-orthogonal-or-negative score as in Part 1, while medical jargon against a general corpus risks the same noise-collapse as Part 2's "novation" example. Since both are stacked here, the safest assumption is that retrieval quality is at least as degraded as the worse of the two individual mismatches, and likely worse -- a general-purpose, English-only model resolves neither problem.

Teach-back: why can't fixing only the language mismatch (switching to a multilingual model) fully resolve this specific query's problem?

23
CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: why isn't a multilingual model just a built-in translator?
Translation converts text from one language into another; a multilingual embedding model never converts anything -- it geometrically aligns meanings from different languages into one shared vector space, so cosine similarity works directly across languages without any intermediate translated text.
Mathematical: what formula does this concept use, and is it new?
The identical cosine similarity formula from Module 04 Concept 03, cos(a,b)=dot(a,b)/(‖a‖‖b‖) -- no new mathematics is introduced; only the underlying vectors change with the model's training data.
Coding: implement and verify both worked scenarios.
Section 12 gives the full from-scratch implementation, reusing Module 04 Concept 03's cosine() unchanged, with asserts reproducing all four Section 10 values.
Debugging: non-English queries against an English corpus return poor results. What's the first thing to check?
Whether the deployed embedding model was actually trained with a cross-lingual alignment objective (like LaBSE's MLM+TLM+translation-ranking recipe) -- a model merely exposed to multiple languages during pretraining, without that specific objective, may still fail to align them.
System design: how would you support a corpus spanning three languages and one specialized domain?
Evaluate whether a single multilingual, domain-adapted model exists or can be fine-tuned for the specific combination, versus running separate per-language or per-domain models with a routing layer -- this decision itself belongs to Concept 10's selection framework, named forward only here.
