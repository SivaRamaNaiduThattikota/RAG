01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Benchmarking and capacity planning -- two words, two jobs

BENCHMARKING

Measures a real recall/latency/memory operating point on a real, fixed dataset -- Concepts 02/03's own SIFT1M numbers are examples of this.

CAPACITY PLANNING

Projects that measured point, plus a real per-component memory formula, onto a different, larger corpus -- to size actual hardware.

First memory: a benchmark tells you what a mechanism costs at the scale it was measured; capacity planning is the arithmetic that carries that cost to your own corpus's own size. Neither one substitutes for the other.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Nine isolated levers need one combined decision

Concepts 01-09 studied exact search, HNSW, IVF, quantization, metadata filtering, tombstones, and sharding each on its own. A real deployment can't tune one lever at a time and hope the rest works out -- it has to combine measured numbers (which mechanism clears the recall bar, which encoding fits in RAM, how many machines that RAM needs) into one hardware plan before the corpus goes live. This concept is the first to do that combining explicitly.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

200 million chunks, one hardware plan

The Oracle RAG project's corpus has grown to N=200,000,000 chunks, d=128 (Concepts 02/03/05's own dimension convention, reused). Target: recall@1 ≥ 85%, lowest available latency, high availability (Qdrant's own cited minimum: at least 3 nodes, replication_factor ≥ 2).

Mechanism (reuses Concepts 02, 03, 06): two cited SIFT1M points clear 85% recall -- HNSW ef_search=16 (0.011 ms/query, 87.40% recall@1) and IVF16384,Flat (0.0538 ms/query, 89.80% recall@1). Concept 06's own marginal-cost-per-recall-point method, applied to this new pair: (0.0538−0.011)/(89.80−87.40) = 0.01783 ms per recall point, a 4.891x latency multiplier for 2.40 extra recall points. HNSW ef_search=16 wins -- the recall gain from IVF isn't worth nearly 5x the latency once the 85% floor is already cleared.

Encoding (reuses Concept 05): SQ8 quantization -- Concept 05's cited 4.00x compression (1 byte/dim vs. Flat's 4 bytes/dim, dimension-independent).

Capacity (new source -- Qdrant's own cited formulas): at replication_factor=2, the whole cluster needs 320.79 GiB / 7 nodes without quantization, or 149.13 GiB / 3 nodes with SQ8 -- bumped to 4 nodes against Qdrant's own stated rough per-node ceiling (~100 million vectors/node). The RAM shrinks 2.15x, not SQ8's own 4.00x per-vector ratio, because the HNSW graph and id-tracker overhead don't compress.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Five real formulas, cited from Qdrant's own capacity-planning page

base = number_of_points × replication_factor -- the real unit every other formula multiplies from. Vector-storage cost = base × dimensions × bytes_per_dim (4 bytes for float32, 1 byte for SQ8 -- Concept 05's own cited ratio, applied here at whole-cluster scale). Graph cost (HNSW) = base × m × 2 × 4 bytes × 1.2, where m is the graph's own connections-per-node parameter (default 16) -- this cost is the same whether or not vectors are quantized, since the graph itself stores integer neighbor ids, not vector values. Id-tracker cost = base × 52 bytes -- fixed per-point bookkeeping overhead, also unaffected by quantization. Headroom and nodes: Qdrant's own docs add 20% headroom on top of the summed RAM/disk totals, then divide by each node's own usable RAM (node_ram × 0.8) and round up to get the node count.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Benchmarking vs. capacity planning -- two jobs, not one

TermThe real difference
BenchmarkingMeasures a recall/latency/memory operating point on a FIXED real dataset, at whatever scale that dataset happens to be (Concepts 02/03's own SIFT1M, 1 million vectors).
Capacity planning (this concept)Projects that measured point, plus a per-component memory formula, onto a DIFFERENT real deployment size -- 200 million chunks here, 200x the benchmark's own scale.
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"A compression ratio divides the node count 1:1."

False -- SQ8's own cited 4.00x per-vector ratio only produced a 2.15x whole-cluster RAM reduction here, because the HNSW graph and id-tracker overhead don't compress.

"A 1-million-vector benchmark holds unchanged at 200x scale."

False -- it's an empirical operating point on one dataset, not a guarantee; Qdrant's own documentation itself recommends testing with a representative sample.

"Skipping the headroom buffer is a safe shortcut."

False -- the +20%/80% buffers exist so a cluster isn't run right at the edge of its own measured capacity.

09 · WHAT YOU SHOULD UNDERSTAND

Readiness check before moving on

Name the two ingredients capacity planning combines

A benchmarked recall/latency/memory operating point on a fixed real dataset, plus a per-component memory formula applied to a different, larger corpus size.

Explain the 4.00x-vs-2.15x gap

SQ8's own cited 4.00x ratio only compresses vector storage; the HNSW graph and id-tracker overhead are fixed and don't compress, so the whole-cluster RAM only shrinks 320.79/149.13 = 2.15x.

Compute a node count from a RAM total and node size

nodes = ceil(total_ram_with_headroom ÷ (node_ram × 0.8)) -- e.g. ceil(149.13 ÷ 51.2) = 3.

04
INTUITION

Sizing a moving truck from a measured box, not a guess

Capacity planning is like sizing a moving truck: you measure one real box (the benchmark -- how much space, in practice, one representative item takes), then multiply by how many boxes you actually have (the corpus size) to pick a truck that fits everything, with a bit of room to spare. Guessing the truck size without measuring a real box, or measuring one box and assuming the truck needs no extra room at all, both fail the same way -- the plan doesn't match reality.

Analogy boundaryThe analogy explains WHY two separate measurements (a real box, a real count) combine into one plan -- not the exact GiB or node numbers, which come from the verified worked example in Section 10.

MATHEMATICS

The marginal-cost equation, reapplied, plus the five cited formulas

marginal cost per recall point = (latency_B − latency_A) ÷ (recall_B − recall_A) = (0.0538 − 0.011) ÷ (89.80 − 87.40) = 0.01783 ms/point
base = N × replication_factor; dense_size = base×d×4; quantized_size = base×d×1; hnsw_size = base×m×2×4×1.2; id_tracker_size = base×52
RAM total = (dense_or_quantized_size + hnsw_size + id_tracker_size) × 1.2; nodes = ceil(RAM total ÷ (node_ram × 0.8))

The marginal-cost equation is Concept 06's own cited method, reapplied here to a new pair of operating points -- not a duplicate of Concept 06's own 7-row table. The five capacity formulas are cited directly from Qdrant's own capacity-planning documentation.

WORKED EXAMPLE

The full five-step decision, N=200,000,000, d=128

Step 1 -- mechanism (reuses Concepts 02, 03, 06). Two cited SIFT1M operating points clear the 85% recall bar: HNSW ef_search=16 (0.011 ms/query, 87.40% recall@1) and IVF16384,Flat (0.0538 ms/query, 89.80% recall@1). Marginal cost = (0.0538−0.011)/(89.80−87.40) = 0.0428/2.40 = 0.01783 ms per recall point, a 4.891x latency multiplier. Decision: HNSW ef_search=16 -- IVF's 2.40-point recall gain costs nearly 5x the latency for a workload whose recall floor is already cleared.

Step 2 -- encoding (reuses Concept 05). SQ8 chosen: Concept 05's cited 4.00x ratio (1 byte/dim vs. Flat's 4 bytes/dim, dimension-independent). PQ's cited 32.00x ratio was considered and set aside -- SQ8 alone already brings the cluster to a small, HA-viable node count, without PQ's added recall risk.

Step 3 -- capacity formula (new source -- Qdrant's own cited page). base = 200,000,000 × 2 = 400,000,000. dense_size = base×128×4 = 190.7349 GiB. quantized_size = base×128×1 = 47.6837 GiB (exactly dense_size/4, Concept 05's own 4.00x ratio holding unchanged at whole-cluster scale). hnsw_size = base×16×2×4×1.2 = 57.2205 GiB (same regardless of quantization). id_tracker_size = base×52 = 19.3715 GiB (same regardless of quantization).

Step 4 -- node count. node_ram=64 GiB, usable=51.2 GiB. No-quant: ceil(320.7922/51.2)=7. SQ8: ceil(149.1308/51.2)=3. Step 5 -- shards and fan-out (reuses Concept 09). shard_number=8 (Qdrant's own cited floor: "create at least two shards per node," 4 nodes×2). replication_factor=2 → 16 total shard-replicas, 4 per node. Fan-out sanity check via Concept 09's own S×local_k formula at the safe rule local_k=k=10: 8×10=80 candidates fanned back per query -- a small, cheap cost next to the RAM/node savings just made.

Preset table (for the lab, same formula, four corpus sizes): N=20,000,000 → no-quant 32.08 GiB/1 node, SQ8 14.91 GiB/1 node (quantization saves RAM but doesn't cross a node threshold here -- a boundary case). CODE

The cited formula and node-count calculator, implemented directly

JAVASCRIPT · FROM SCRATCH -- REPRODUCES THIS CONCEPT'S OWN PRESET TABLE EXACTLY 
// Qdrant's own cited capacity-planning formulas, applied directly.
function capacityPlan(n, replicationFactor, d, m, nodeRam, quantized) {
  const base = n * replicationFactor;
  const bytesPerDim = quantized ? 1 : 4;
  const vectorBytes = base * d * bytesPerDim;
  const hnswBytes = base * m * 2 * 4 * 1.2;
  const idTrackerBytes = base * 52;
  const GiB = 1024 ** 3;
  const totalGiB = ((vectorBytes + hnswBytes + idTrackerBytes) / GiB) * 1.2;
  const usableRam = nodeRam * 0.8;
  const nodes = Math.ceil(totalGiB / usableRam);
  return { vectorGiB: vectorBytes / GiB, hnswGiB: hnswBytes / GiB, idTrackerGiB: idTrackerBytes / GiB, totalGiB, nodes };
}

const d = 128, m = 16, replicationFactor = 2, nodeRam = 64;

const noQuant200M = capacityPlan(200_000_000, replicationFactor, d, m, nodeRam, false);
const sq8_200M = capacityPlan(200_000_000, replicationFactor, d, m, nodeRam, true);

console.assert(Math.abs(noQuant200M.totalGiB - 320.7922) < 0.01, "no-quant total matches 320.79 GiB");
console.assert(noQuant200M.nodes === 7, "no-quant needs 7 nodes");
console.assert(Math.abs(sq8_200M.totalGiB - 149.1308) < 0.01, "SQ8 total matches 149.13 GiB");
console.assert(sq8_200M.nodes === 3, "SQ8 needs 3 nodes (ceil, before the 100M/node ceiling bump)");
console.assert(Math.abs(noQuant200M.totalGiB / sq8_200M.totalGiB - 2.151) < 0.01, "whole-cluster reduction is ~2.15x, not SQ8's own 4.00x per-vector ratio");

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

UNDERSIZED CLUSTER

Headroom skipped

Symptom: a cluster sized to the raw formula total, with no +20% buffer, runs out of memory the first time load spikes above average.

STALE-BENCHMARK ASSUMPTION

Bench at 1M, deploy at 500M unverified

Symptom: a recall number measured at SIFT1M scale is assumed to hold unchanged at 500x the corpus size, without re-testing on a representative sample.

WRONG SHARD COUNT

Too few, or too many

Symptom: too few shards blocks growth without a full reshard (Qdrant's own cited "cannot be changed without recreating the collection" rule); too many wastes per-shard overhead on a small cluster.

Misconceptions

Incorrect beliefCorrection

18
REALITY

Production implications

  • Capacity planning happens pre-launch, and gets re-validated as the corpus actually grows -- it is not a one-time calculation.
  • Resharding an existing collection is not free -- Qdrant's own documentation states shard_number "cannot be changed without recreating the collection," which is exactly why Concept 09's own sharding decision and this concept's own node-count decision are made together, up front.
  • Headroom is a real operational buffer against load spikes, not padding to be trimmed under budget pressure.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: how does benchmarking differ from capacity planning?
Benchmarking measures a real operating point on a fixed dataset at whatever scale that dataset happens to be; capacity planning projects that measured point, plus a per-component memory formula, onto a different, usually much larger, deployment size.
Mathematical: recompute the node count at a new N, say 100,000,000, with SQ8.
base = 100,000,000×2=200,000,000; total RAM with SQ8 scales linearly from the N=200,000,000 SQ8 total (149.1308 GiB) by a factor of 0.5, giving ≈74.57 GiB; ceil(74.57/51.2) = 2 nodes.
Coding: extend the calculator in Section 12 with a PQ option.
