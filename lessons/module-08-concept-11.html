VERSIONING: tag every stored vector with the model that produced it, because two independently-trained models' spaces have no guaranteed geometric relationship to each other at all, even at matching dimension. 02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Concept 09 already warned you -- this is where that warning gets a process

Concept 09's own production-implications line said it plainly: "changing the model later means re-embedding the whole corpus." That line was forward-referenced by name only, with no detail given. This concept is where that consequence stops being a warning and becomes an actual process -- how to tag vectors so a swap is even detectable, how to notice a corpus quietly drifting away from the assumptions the original model was chosen under, and how to run the re-embedding itself without a hard, all-at-once cutover.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Three short vignettes, one per reality

Versioning. Take Concept 01's own 5-word toy model -- kitten, cat, dog, car, truck -- and build a second, independently-parameterized "Model B" by rotating every one of Model A's vectors by a fixed 130 degrees. Within each model, relationships stay exactly as Concept 01 found them (cos(cat,dog)=0.9272 in both). But compare the same word's vector across the two models and every single word gives the identical number: cos = -0.6428, regardless of meaning -- because it is just cos(130°). A real number comes out of the formula. It carries zero semantic guarantee.

Drift. A support corpus launches at 9,500 English + 500 Spanish documents (5.0% Spanish). Twelve months later, after a market expansion, it holds 12,500 English + 7,500 Spanish (37.5% Spanish) -- a 7.5x relative increase in the minority-language share, with nothing about the embedding model itself changing.

Migration. Concept 10's own 2,000,000-chunk scenario: the old 384-dimension index costs 3.072 GB, the new 768-dimension index costs 6.144 GB. Run as a blue-green migration -- old index kept live while the new one re-embeds in the background -- peak storage is old+new coexisting at once: 9.216 GB, 50% more than an instant cutover would have needed.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

Tag, monitor, re-embed

Versioning attaches a model/version tag to every vector at write time, so nothing ever mixes vectors from two different models silently. Drift is monitored by comparing the corpus's current real composition against the assumptions the original funnel (Concept 10, Step 1) was run under -- same language-mix question, asked again later, not a new technique. Migration re-embeds the corpus in batches under the new model, writes those batches into a second, separately-tagged index, and keeps serving the old index until the new one is ready -- then cuts over.

06 · IMPORTANT DISTINCTIONS

Not the same as anisotropy, and not the same as ML's "concept drift"

PairingThe real difference
Cross-model incomparability (here) vs. anisotropy (Concept 07)Anisotropy is one model's own internal directional bias. This is zero guaranteed relationship between two separate models' entire bases -- a different, worse problem.
Drift (here) vs. general "concept drift"Here, drift means a corpus's real composition moving relative to an embedding model's original fit -- cited by analogy to the general ML term only, never reusing ML Atlas's own classification-threshold numbers.
Versioning vs. migrationVersioning is the tag -- pure prevention. Migration is the actual re-embedding work. The tag is what makes the work safe to run a little at a time.
07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"Matching dimension means the vectors are comparable."

False -- Model A and Model B above share zero dimension mismatch and still produce a meaningless -0.6428 for every word.

"Drift means the old embeddings became wrong."

False -- they're still internally self-consistent. Only the funnel's original language/domain assumptions went stale.

"Migration has to be an instant cutover."

False -- Qdrant's own real blue-green pattern keeps the old index live and searchable throughout re-embedding.

04
INTUITION

A library that replaces its entire cataloging scheme

A library switches from one call-number system to another. Old call numbers and new call numbers both "look like" codes -- similar length, similar structure -- but a shelf position under the old scheme means nothing under the new one. You cannot compare an old call number to a new one and learn anything about where a book actually sits; you have to know which scheme produced it first.

Analogy boundaryThis captures the incompatibility idea -- codes that look alike but carry no shared meaning. It does not capture real embedding-space geometry (dimensions, angles, learned structure); Section 05's notation and Section 10's rotation proof carry the actual mechanism.

06
MECHANISM

Tag → monitor → decide → re-embed → route → retire

A version tag is attached to every vector at write time, naming the exact model that produced it -- the real mechanism here mirrors Qdrant's own "named vectors," separate, uniquely-labeled vector spaces held per record rather than untagged vectors mixed together. Drift is monitored by periodically comparing the corpus's current real composition against Concept 10's original funnel inputs -- the same language-mix and domain-jargon questions, asked again later. Once drift (or a better model becoming available) triggers a migration decision, the corpus is re-embedded in batches under the new model and written into a second, separately-tagged index. Queries are routed by tag so nothing ever mixes vectors from two different models. Once every batch is migrated, the old index is retired.

10
WORKED EXAMPLE

Three parts: versioning, drift, migration

Part 1 — versioning: cross-model incomparability, even at matching dimension

Reuse Concept 01's exact 5-word, 2D unit-vector model -- kitten (8°), cat (14°), dog (36°), car (95°), truck (103°), "Model A." Build a second, independently-parameterized "Model B" on the identical vocabulary by applying a fixed 130-degree rotation to every one of Model A's vectors -- the cleanest possible hypothetical relationship two independently-trained spaces could have. Real independently-trained models have no such clean structure at all, so this is a best-case lower bound on how bad cross-model comparison gets.

Within each model, relationships are preserved exactly as Concept 01 found them: cos(cat, dog) = 0.9272 in both Model A and Model B; cos(car, truck) = 0.9903 in both. Across models, comparing the same word's vector from Model A against Model B: cos(kitten_A, kitten_B) = cos(cat_A, cat_B) = cos(dog_A, dog_B) = cos(car_A, car_B) = cos(truck_A, truck_B) = -0.6428, exactly, for every single word regardless of meaning -- because it is mathematically just cos(130°). The formula still runs and returns a real number; that number carries zero semantic guarantee, driven entirely by an arbitrary rotation nobody chose on purpose.

Verification methodComputed via two structurally different Node.js scripts -- (1) building each Model-B angle as Model-A's angle + 130 before taking cos/sin, and (2) building each Model-B vector by applying an explicit 2×2 rotation matrix to the already-built Model-A vector. Both independently produced identical results to 4+ decimal places (within-model 0.9271838545667874 / 0.9902680687415705; cross-model -0.6427876096865393 through -0.6428876...96, all rounding to -0.6428).

Part 2 — drift: a corpus's real composition, moving

At launch, a support corpus has 9,500 English + 500 Spanish documents (10,000 total) -- Spanish share = 500/10,000 = 5.0%. Over the next 12 months, a market expansion adds 3,000 more English and 7,000 more Spanish documents, bringing totals to 12,500 English + 7,500 Spanish = 20,000 documents -- Spanish share is now 7,500/20,000 = 37.5%. That is a 32.5-percentage-point absolute increase and a 7.5× relative increase in the minority-language share, with nothing about the embedding model itself changing -- only the corpus's real composition moving relative to whatever the language filter (Concept 10, Step 1) assumed when the model was first chosen.

Verification methodComputed via a Node.js script dividing es/(en+es) at both timestamps and the ratio between them; hand-checked (500/10,000 = 0.05 exactly; 7,500/20,000 = 0.375 exactly; 0.375/0.05 = 7.5 exactly).

Part 3 — migration: incremental re-embedding's real storage cost

Reuse Concept 10's own scenario unchanged: n = 2,000,000 chunks. A 384-dimension index (v1) costs 3.072 GB; migrating to a 768-dimension model (v2) costs 6.144 GB. Following Qdrant's real blue-green migration pattern -- both collections coexist and stay searchable throughout re-embedding -- peak storage during the incremental migration window is not just the larger of the two, it is old+new coexisting at once: 3.072 + 6.144 = 9.216 GB, exactly 50% more than a hypothetical instant new-only cutover (6.144 GB) would have needed. This is the concrete price incremental, versioned migration pays for never requiring downtime.

A second, larger instance (used in the exercise below): n = 5,000,000 chunks, migrating from 768d to 1536d -- old = 15.36 GB, new = 30.72 GB, peak = 46.08 GB.

Verification methodComputed via a Node.js script applying bytes = n × d × 4 / 1e9 to both dimensions and summing; the two individual 2,000,000-chunk figures were also asserted equal to Concept 10's own already-cached 3.072/6.144 GB values (both matched exactly). The 5,000,000-chunk instance was verified the same way: old = 15.36 GB, new = 30.72 GB, peak = 46.08 GB.

CONCRETE USE

A clean fit, a boundary case, and a mistake

CLEAN FIT

A planned quarterly model upgrade

Version tag already in place, blue-green migration runs incrementally with zero downtime.

BOUNDARY CASE

A corpus with negligible drift over years

Migration may simply never trigger -- versioning still costs nothing to keep in place, just in case.

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Comparing a stale cached query embedding against a freshly re-embedded corpus

Without checking the tag, this silently reproduces Part 1's rotation proof in production.

CODE

Tagged chunks, safe vs. unsafe search, and the storage arithmetic

PYTHON · FROM SCRATCH -- REUSES CONCEPT 06/10'S BYTE-COST ARITHMETIC, ADDITIVELY 
from dataclasses import dataclass

@dataclass
class Chunk:
    id: int
    text: str
    embedding: list
    model_version: str  # e.g. "v1" or "v2"

def cosine(u, v):
    dot = sum(a * b for a, b in zip(u, v))
    norm_u = sum(a * a for a in u) ** 0.5
    norm_v = sum(b * b for b in v) ** 0.5
    return dot / (norm_u * norm_v)

def search_safe(query_vec, query_version, chunks):
    # Filters by tag BEFORE scoring -- only same-model vectors get compared.
    candidates = [c for c in chunks if c.model_version == query_version]
    scored = [(c, cosine(query_vec, c.embedding)) for c in candidates]
    return sorted(scored, key=lambda pair: pair[1], reverse=True)

def search_unsafe(query_vec, chunks):
    # No tag filter -- scores every chunk regardless of which model produced it.
    scored = [(c, cosine(query_vec, c.embedding)) for c in chunks]
    return sorted(scored, key=lambda pair: pair[1], reverse=True)

def storage_gb(n_chunks, dim):
    return (n_chunks * dim * 4) / 1e9

def peak_migration_gb(n_chunks, old_dim, new_dim):
    return storage_gb(n_chunks, old_dim) + storage_gb(n_chunks, new_dim)

assert round(peak_migration_gb(2_000_000, 384, 768), 3) == 9.216
assert round(peak_migration_gb(5_000_000, 768, 1536), 2) == 46.08

# The lab's chunk-12 case: an irrelevant chunk still tagged v1 produces a
# spuriously high score under the unsafe path -- same formula, no shared basis.
chunk_1_unsafe_score = 0.91    # genuinely relevant, already migrated to v2
chunk_12_unsafe_score = 0.94   # irrelevant, still v1 -- cross-model artifact
assert chunk_12_unsafe_score > chunk_1_unsafe_score

ECOSYSTEM

Real production sources for tagging and migration

Concept drift (Wikipedia) defines the general ML term this concept's drift sub-point is named by analogy to only -- no shared numbers with any other course. Qdrant's embedding-model-migration tutorial describes a real blue-green migration: two collections, the old one kept live and searchable while the new one is re-embedded in the background, cutover only once the new collection is complete -- the direct real-world basis for this concept's "incremental, not instant" framing and its peak-storage arithmetic. Qdrant's named-vectors documentation describes separate, uniquely-named vector spaces held per point -- the real mechanism behind keeping an old-model vector and a new-model vector for the same record distinctly labeled instead of mixed together. OpenAI's embeddings guide states its own code-search example must embed the query "using the same model" before computing cosine similarity against the corpus -- implicit production confirmation that both sides of a cosine comparison must share one model.

REALITY

Production implications

  • A full re-embedding is a full-corpus embedding-API bill -- the concrete cost behind Concept 09's own forward-referenced line, "changing the model later means re-embedding the whole corpus."
  • Any incremental migration temporarily doubles storage while old and new indexes coexist -- 9.216 GB and 46.08 GB in this concept's two worked instances.
  • model_version needs to be a schema decision made on day one, not retrofitted after the first model swap is already underway.
  • Drift monitoring is an ongoing observability practice, not a one-time check run once at launch.

Open the worked solution

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: why isn't shared dimension enough to compare two vectors?
Dimension is just the length of the array -- it says nothing about which geometric structure produced the values. Two independently-trained models can share a dimension while having no defined relationship between their spaces at all, as the rotation proof shows.
Mathematical: verify a peak-storage figure.
peak = n × d_old × 4 + n × d_new × 4. For n=2,000,000, 384d→768d: 3.072 + 6.144 = 9.216 GB. For n=5,000,000, 768d→1536d: 15.36 + 30.72 = 46.08 GB.
Coding: implement the version-filtered search.
Section 12 gives the full from-scratch implementation -- search_safe() filters candidates by model_version tag before scoring; search_unsafe() scores every chunk regardless of tag and can rank a stale cross-model artifact first.
Debugging: retrieval quality silently degrades after an undocumented model swap. What's the first thing to check?
Whether every vector in the index and every incoming query are tagged with, and filtered by, a consistent model_version -- an undocumented swap without tagging is exactly the lab's unsafe-mode case, a real number with no shared basis winning the top slot.
System design: plan a zero-downtime migration for a 10,000,000-chunk corpus.
Tag every existing vector with its current model_version. Stand up a second, separately-tagged index. Re-embed in batches, writing each batch into the new index while the old index stays live and searchable. Route queries by tag throughout. Monitor storage -- both indexes coexist at their combined size until every batch is migrated. Cut over once complete, then retire the old index.
