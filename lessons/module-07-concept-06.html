Overlap fixes the cut —
until it doesn't.

PROGRESSIVE BEGINNER CORE

A stride adjustment, not a new method

Concepts 02 through 05 picked a splitting method and a size. This concept asks a narrower question about the boundary itself: what if neighboring chunks shared a little content across it, so a fact that happens to straddle the cut doesn't lose meaning on either side?

01 · CONCEPT FIRST

Overlap means consecutive chunks share characters across the boundary

THIS CONCEPT

Chunk k starts at k·stride instead of k·chunk_size (Concept 02's own notation). When stride is smaller than chunk_size, neighboring chunks share their trailing/leading characters. Concept 02's own worked examples all used stride = chunk_size — zero overlap, the special case this concept now generalizes past.

NOT THIS CONCEPT

Parent-document, parent-child and small-to-big retrieval (Concept 07) and hierarchical/late chunking (Concept 08) — the two structural escapes from this same trade-off, named forward only here.

First memory: overlap doesn't split differently or resize a chunk — it's a stride adjustment riding on top of whichever method and size Concepts 02-05 already chose.
02 · WHY IT EXISTS

Concept 05 already named this fix

Concept 05's own closing line named chunk overlap as one of two forthcoming mitigations for the size trade-off it introduced. This concept builds it. The problem it targets: a fact or sentence that happens to straddle a chunk boundary can lose meaning if the split shares nothing with the chunk on either side — Concept 02's own blind, character-level cut, now happening to land inside a whole fact instead of just one word.

03 · CONCRETE EXAMPLE

Oracle Order Management's grace-period fact, cut mid-word

Passage (297 characters): "Oracle Order Management lets a customer cancel an order within a fixed period after purchase. The order-cancellation grace period is 14 days from the ship date. Once it elapses, the order status locks to CLOSED and refunds stop processing automatically. Support agents must file a manual override." Target fact: "The order-cancellation grace period is 14 days from the ship date." — 66 characters, at position [94, 160).

At chunk_size N=100 with zero overlap (stride=100, Concept 02's own special case), chunk 1 ends "...The or" and chunk 2 begins "der-cancellation grace period..." — a blind character-level cut lands mid-word inside "order-cancellation." Neither chunk alone states the complete fact. Shrinking the stride to 90 (a 10% overlap) fixes it: chunk 2 now runs [90,190), which fully covers the fact's [94,160) span.

04 · TECHNICAL MECHANISM

stride = chunk_size − overlap_size, and the cost of shrinking it

SettingChunksChars storedExtra vs. original 297Fact status
0% overlap (stride=100)3297+0.00%SPLIT
10% overlap (stride=90)4 (+33% embedding calls)327+10.10%CONTAINED

Shrinking the stride below chunk_size makes neighboring chunks share characters — that's what fixes the cut. But every shared character gets stored and embedded twice. Fixing this one fact with the cheapest overlap that works costs 10.10% more storage and 33% more embedding calls at ingestion time, before a single query is ever asked.

07 · COMMON MISTAKES

What beginners often assume, and shouldn't

"More overlap is always safer."

False, and not just a little false — the advanced section derives a case where 50% overlap fails after 40% succeeded on the identical fact. Held here as a simplified pointer; Section 09 proves it.

"Overlap is free."

False — quantified above. Even the cheapest fix that works on this one fact costs +10.10% storage and +33% more embedding calls.

"Overlap fixes every boundary-loss case."

False — a longer fact needs proportionally more overlap to guarantee coverage, or a structural fix (Concept 07's parent-child retrieval, named forward) instead of paying for ever-larger overlap.

06
MECHANISM

Two lanes: zero overlap vs. positive overlap

ZERO-OVERLAP LANE

stride = chunk_size (Concept 02's own case). Every character is stored and embedded exactly once. A fact straddling a boundary is split with nothing shared on either side.

POSITIVE-OVERLAP LANE

stride < chunk_size. Characters in the shared span are stored and embedded in both neighboring chunks — raising num_chunks and total stored/embedded characters beyond L, in exchange for a chance (not a guarantee, below the threshold) that a boundary-straddling fact lands whole inside one chunk.

07
VISUALIZATION

The guarantee zone on the overlap_size axis

Every point below overlap_size=66 is "positional luck" for this particular fact — it happened to work at 10/20/30/40% and happened to fail again at 50%. Only overlap_size=66, meeting the fact's own 66-character length, enters the shaded zone where containment is certain regardless of exactly where the fact sits.

09
MATHEMATICS

The coverage guarantee: overlap_size ≥ fact_length

overlap_size = N − stride
Guarantee: a fact of length ℓ, placed anywhere in the document's interior, lands fully inside some chunk regardless of position ⇔ overlap_size ≥ ℓ (equivalently stride ≤ N − ℓ)

Chunk starts are multiples of stride. For a fact spanning [a, a+ℓ), a chunk containing it needs its own start s to fall in the interval [a+ℓ−N, a] — an interval of length N−ℓ. That interval is guaranteed to contain a multiple of stride, for ANY placement of a, exactly when N−ℓ ≥ stride, i.e. overlap_size = N−stride ≥ ℓ. For this lesson's fact, ℓ=66, so the guarantee threshold is overlap_size ≥ 66, i.e. stride ≤ 34 — exactly the stride=34 row in Section 10's table, which succeeds, while every overlap between 10% and 50% is positional luck: none of those overlap_sizes (10, 20, 30, 40, 50) reach the 66-character threshold this specific fact needs, so their pass/pass/pass/pass/FAIL pattern is exactly what the formula predicts — not "more is always safer."

Interior assumptionThis derivation assumes the fact sits away from the document's very start or its final, possibly-shorter clipped chunk. This lesson's fact at [94,160) of a 297-character document is safely interior, so the numbers above aren't affected — but the guarantee as stated doesn't unconditionally cover a fact sitting right at position 0 or inside the last chunk.

11
CONCRETE USE

A clean fit, a boundary case, a mistake

CLEAN FIT

Short FAQ-style facts

A modest overlap (LlamaIndex's ~2%, LangChain's ~5%) is usually enough in practice, because most atomic facts are much shorter than a full chunk.

BOUNDARY CASE

A long clause or definition

When a fact's own length approaches or exceeds a modest overlap budget, the guarantee threshold demands an overlap large enough to feel expensive — or a structural fix (Concept 07, named forward).

MISTAKE TO AVOID

Setting a large blanket overlap "to be safe"

Paying 133% extra storage (this lesson's 66% row) still isn't certain for a fact longer than 66 characters in this passage — "large" isn't the same as "checked against the formula."

12
CODE

The sliding-window chunker and containment checker, from scratch

PYTHON · NO LIBRARIES 
def chunk_fixed(text, n, stride):
    chunks = []
    start = 0
    while start < len(text):
        end = min(start + n, len(text))
        chunks.append((start, end))
        if end == len(text):
            break
        start += stride
    return chunks

def contains_fact(chunks, fact_start, fact_end):
    return any(c[0] <= fact_start and c[1] >= fact_end for c in chunks)

def cost(chunks, doc_len):
    total_stored = sum(end - start for start, end in chunks)
    extra_pct = (total_stored - doc_len) / doc_len * 100
    return len(chunks), total_stored, extra_pct

PASSAGE = ("Oracle Order Management lets a customer cancel an order within a "
           "fixed period after purchase. The order-cancellation grace period "
           "is 14 days from the ship date. Once it elapses, the order status "
           "locks to CLOSED and refunds stop processing automatically. "
           "Support agents must file a manual override.")
FACT = "The order-cancellation grace period is 14 days from the ship date."
fact_start = PASSAGE.index(FACT)
fact_end = fact_start + len(FACT)
N = 100

for stride in (100, 90, 50, 34):
    chunks = chunk_fixed(PASSAGE, N, stride)
    n_chunks, stored, extra_pct = cost(chunks, len(PASSAGE))
    contained = contains_fact(chunks, fact_start, fact_end)
    print(stride, n_chunks, stored, round(extra_pct, 2), contained)

assert contains_fact(chunk_fixed(PASSAGE, N, 100), fact_start, fact_end) is False
assert contains_fact(chunk_fixed(PASSAGE, N, 90), fact_start, fact_end) is True
assert contains_fact(chunk_fixed(PASSAGE, N, 50), fact_start, fact_end) is False
assert contains_fact(chunk_fixed(PASSAGE, N, 34), fact_start, fact_end) is True
assert (N - 34) >= len(FACT)  # 66 >= 66 -- the guarantee condition, exactly met

ECOSYSTEM

Real defaults sit far below this lesson's demonstration overlaps

LlamaIndex's SentenceSplitter ships DEFAULT_CHUNK_OVERLAP = 20 tokens against DEFAULT_CHUNK_SIZE = 1024 tokens (confirmed directly from source) — an overlap_ratio of about 2%. LangChain's TextSplitter defaults chunk_overlap to 200 characters against a 4000-character chunk_size (confirmed directly from source) — about 5% — and its constructor raises a ValueError if chunk_overlap exceeds chunk_size, a real production guardrail confirming overlap must stay smaller than chunk_size (stride must stay positive). Both real defaults are "cheap, partial risk reduction" in practice, not "the guarantee" this lesson derives — production systems accept some residual boundary-loss risk rather than paying this lesson's 66% row for every corpus.

16
DIAGNOSIS

Failure modes

INSUFFICIENT OVERLAP

Still splits, unpredictably

Any overlap_size below the fact's own length is positional luck, not protection — it can pass on one fact and fail on the next.

UNCHECKED "MORE IS SAFER"

A larger overlap regresses on the same fact

This lesson's own 40%→50% row: increasing overlap made containment fail again after it had already succeeded.

ONE FIXED OVERLAP, VARYING FACT LENGTHS

A single overlap_size across a heterogeneous corpus

An overlap sized for short facts leaves long facts unprotected — the guarantee is per-fact-length, not a corpus-wide constant.

Incorrect beliefCorrection

18
REALITY

Production implications

  • Redundant embedding calls and index storage both compound with corpus size — a fixed overlap_ratio on a 10x larger corpus means 10x the redundant characters, not a fixed cost.
  • Unlike Concept 05's per-query dilution cost, overlap's redundancy cost is paid entirely at ingestion time, regardless of which queries are ever asked against the corpus.
  • overlap_size is normally tuned empirically against a corpus's own known maximum atomic-fact length, not chosen as a round percentage — Section 09's formula is the actual tuning target.
19
RECALL

Knowledge check

Why does 50% overlap fail on this fact while 40% overlap succeeded?
Neither overlap_size (40 or 50) reaches the fact's 66-character guarantee threshold, so both results are positional luck rather than certainty. This particular fact's position happened to align with a chunk boundary at 50% overlap and not at 40% — the guarantee formula doesn't predict "more is safer" below the threshold, so this isn't a contradiction, it's exactly what the formula says can happen.
What does the coverage guarantee formula actually require?
overlap_size ≥ fact_length (equivalently stride ≤ chunk_size − fact_length), for a fact sitting in the document's interior. Only when the shared span between neighboring chunks is at least as long as the fact itself is containment certain regardless of exactly where that fact sits.
Why is overlap's redundancy cost paid regardless of which queries are ever asked?
Overlap is applied once, at ingestion time, before any query exists — every overlapping character is stored and embedded then, unconditionally. This is different from Concept 05's dilution cost, which only shows up per query at retrieval time.

FEEDBACK

Solution and teach-back

Open the worked solution
At stride=80 (0% overlap), chunks are [0,80), [80,160), [160,219). The fact spans [87,139), which falls entirely inside chunk 2's range [80,160) — CONTAINED. At stride=50 (30% overlap, overlap_size=30), chunks are [0,80), [50,130), [100,180), [150,219). No single chunk covers all of [87,139): chunk 2 ends at 130, nine characters short of the fact's own end at 139 — SPLIT. So zero overlap happens to contain this fact while a real 30% overlap does not. The guarantee formula explains why neither result is surprising: the fact is 52 characters long, so the real threshold is overlap_size ≥ 52 (stride ≤ 28). Neither stride=80 (overlap_size=0) nor stride=50 (overlap_size=30) reaches that threshold, so both outcomes are positional luck — one landed as contained, one didn't, and the formula never claimed overlap_size=30 would help here. This is the same lesson as Section 10's 40%→50% row, in a fresh passage: below the threshold, containment is luck in either direction, not a monotonic function of overlap.

CAREER PRACTICE

Interview questions

Conceptual: why isn't "just add more overlap" a reliable fix for boundary loss?
Below the guarantee threshold (overlap_size ≥ fact_length), containment depends on exactly where the fact happens to sit relative to chunk boundaries — a matter of position, not overlap magnitude. This lesson's own 40%→50% regression shows a larger overlap failing where a smaller one succeeded on the identical fact.
Mathematical: derive the exact condition under which overlap guarantees containment.
Chunk starts are multiples of stride. A chunk containing a fact spanning [a, a+ℓ) needs a start s in [a+ℓ−N, a], an interval of length N−ℓ. That interval contains a multiple of stride for every possible a exactly when N−ℓ ≥ stride, i.e. overlap_size = N−stride ≥ ℓ.
Coding: implement a fact-containment checker and a cost calculator for a given stride.
chunk_fixed(text, N, stride) produces (start, end) spans via a sliding window; contains_fact checks whether any span fully covers [fact_start, fact_end); cost() sums span lengths for total_stored and derives extra_pct against the raw document length — Section 12 gives the full from-scratch implementation.
Debugging: a corpus still loses a fact despite a 30% chunk overlap. What do you check first?
Whether overlap_size (0.30 × chunk_size) actually meets or exceeds that fact's own character length — if the fact is longer than the overlap, the failure is exactly what the coverage formula predicts, not a bug in the chunker.
System design: how would you choose overlap_size for a corpus with a known maximum atomic-fact length?
Set overlap_size at or above that maximum length to satisfy the guarantee for every fact in the corpus, then weigh the resulting storage/embedding-call cost against a structural alternative (Concept 07's parent-child retrieval) if that overlap turns out too expensive to justify corpus-wide.
